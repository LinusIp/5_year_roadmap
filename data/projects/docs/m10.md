# First lit, textured OpenGL scene with a camera

**Goal.** An OpenGL 3.3 core scene of several textured objects lit by directional, point and spot lights with diffuse and specular maps, explored with a fly camera at the display's refresh rate, built on your own shader, mesh and camera classes.

**Why.** This is the step from following LearnOpenGL to owning a renderer. It builds OpenGL, GLSL and linear algebra in practice, and the nodes `game-developer/opengl`, `game-developer/glsl`, `game-developer/shader`, `game-developer/texture`, `game-developer/diffuse`, `game-developer/specular`, `game-developer/directional`, `game-developer/point`, `game-developer/spot` and `game-developer/perspective`.

**Scope.** MVP: a Shader class (compile, link, uniform cache, hot reload), Mesh (VAO, VBO, EBO), Texture, a Camera (yaw, pitch, WASD, scroll zoom), Blinn-Phong lighting with diffuse and specular maps, and a scene of at least 10 objects. Stretch: OBJ loading with Assimp, normal mapping, a skybox, a shadow map for the directional light, a Dear ImGui panel for light parameters.

**Architecture.** The application owns the window and the loop. Each frame it feeds input to the camera, computes the view and projection matrices, and draws each object: bind the shader, set the model, view and projection matrices and the normal matrix, bind the material textures, upload the lights, draw. Lights are plain structs uploaded as uniform arrays or a uniform buffer.

```
input ─▶ Camera ─▶ view, proj ──┐
Scene: [Mesh + Material + model] ─▶ Renderer ─▶ Shader (Blinn-Phong) ─▶ framebuffer
Lights: dir, points[], spot ────┘
```

**Stack.** C++17, OpenGL 3.3 core, GLFW, glad, GLM, stb_image, CMake, RenderDoc for frame debugging.

**Milestones.** Window, loop and a Shader class with hot reload (5 h) · Mesh, Texture and a textured cube (5 h) · fly camera with view and projection (4 h) · Phong lighting with material maps (6 h) · directional, point and spot lights in one scene (6 h) · timing, screenshot, clip and README (4 h). Total 30 h.

**Done when.** No class is pasted wholesale from the tutorial; the README notes where each idea came from and what you changed. The scene shows all three light types on at least 10 textured objects with diffuse and specular maps. It holds the display's refresh rate (60 or 144 Hz) at 1080p with vsync on, and frame time is reported with vsync off. A RenderDoc capture shows no per-frame texture or buffer re-uploads. A screenshot and a short clip are in the README.

**Measure and report.** Frame time p50 and p99 at 1080p with vsync off; draw calls and triangles per frame; the GPU used; one RenderDoc capture, explained.

**Pitfalls.** Normals transformed by the model matrix instead of its inverse transpose, which breaks lighting under non-uniform scale. Textures upside down because stb_image and OpenGL disagree on the origin. Lighting computed on sRGB colours without linearising. A camera built from Euler angles that flips at the poles. Uniforms set before the program is bound.

**References.** learnopengl, gamemath-3d-primer, rtr4, utah-cs6610, roadmap-game-developer.

**Write-up.** A hero screenshot and a 15-second clip, a class diagram, the frame-time table, and the feature you would add first (shadows or normal maps).
