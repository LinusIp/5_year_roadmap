# DC or BLDC motor driver with PID speed control

**Goal.** A brushed DC motor (or a small BLDC) driven through an H-bridge with current sensing and an encoder, whose electrical and mechanical parameters you identified from measurements, under a PI(D) speed loop designed on that model, with current limiting and stall protection tested on purpose.

**Why.** Skills: power stages (H-bridges, gate drive, flyback paths), PWM, system identification, and PID tuning on a model rather than by feel. It powers the inverted pendulum of `m45` and the year 4 capstone `cap-y4`. Resources: `brian-douglas-control`, `feedback-systems`, `mit-2-004`.

**Scope.** MVP: brushed DC gearmotor with a quadrature encoder, a driver IC at 20 kHz PWM, a shunt and current-sense amplifier sampled in sync with the PWM, identification of R, L, back-EMF constant, inertia and friction, a PI speed loop at 1 kHz with anti-windup, a current limit and stall detection. Stretch: an inner current loop, a BLDC with Hall sensors and six-step commutation, a serial tuning GUI with live step plots.

**Architecture.** The encoder timer gives position; speed comes from the count difference per control period. The control ISR runs at 1 kHz: speed error into the PI controller, output clamped by the current limiter, then into the PWM duty. The ADC samples the shunt at the PWM centre. A supervisor watches current and speed and disables the bridge on a stall.

```
speed ref ─▶(+)─▶ PI ─▶ current limit ─▶ PWM ─▶ H-bridge ─▶ motor ─▶ encoder
             ▲─                               │ shunt              │
             │                                 └─▶ ADC ─▶ supervisor (stall trip)
             └──────────── speed estimate ◀────────────────────────┘
```

**Stack.** C on your project 39 drivers (or Rust), timer encoder mode and centre-aligned PWM, Python with NumPy, SciPy and python-control for identification and design, a serial telemetry link logging at 1 kHz, the bench scope.

**Parts and budget.**
- 12 V brushed gearmotor with a quadrature encoder, e.g. a 37 mm class motor ($25–50)
- Motor driver: DRV8871 or VNH5019 module, or a MOSFET bridge with a gate driver ($8–40)
- Current-sense shunt and amplifier (INA240 or INA180 class) ($5–15)
- Microcontroller board, reused from project 39 ($0–25)
- Bench supply with current limit, reused ($0)
- Flyback and TVS diodes, bulk capacitor, connectors ($3–8)
- Printed motor mount and a flywheel ($2–5)
- Total: about $45–145

**Milestones.** Power stage, 20 kHz PWM, encoder timer and current sensing (8 h) · system identification of R, L, back-EMF constant, inertia and friction (8 h) · model and PI design in Python (6 h) · speed loop firmware and step tests against prediction (8 h) · current limit and stall protection, tested on purpose (5 h) · write-up (5 h). Total 40 h.

**Done when.** The identified model predicts the open-loop speed step within 10 % at three voltages. The closed-loop step response matches the predicted one: overshoot within 5 percentage points and settling time within 20 % (for example a design of 5 % overshoot and 150 ms settling). Steady-state speed error is under 1 % from 20 % to 100 % of rated speed. A deliberate stall holds current at the set limit within 10 % and trips the bridge within 200 ms, repeated five times without damage.

**Measure and report.** Identified R, L, Ke, J and friction with how each was measured; predicted and measured step responses overlaid; rise time, overshoot and settling time; gain and phase margin of the designed loop; current ripple at 20 kHz in mA; stall trip time and peak current.

**Pitfalls.** Sampling the shunt during switching edges instead of at the PWM centre. Integrator windup when the current limit saturates the output. Speed estimates from encoder counts that are too coarse at low speed (use a longer window or period measurement). Missing a flyback path, so the bridge dies on the first direction change. Identifying inertia with the gearbox's friction lumped in and never checking.

**References.** brian-douglas-control, feedback-systems, mit-2-004, mit-6-302, cu-boulder-power-electronics, art-of-electronics, m39.

**Write-up.** A photo of the rig, the identification method and results, the predicted-against-measured step plot, the stall test captured on the scope, and a paragraph on what an inner current loop would buy.
