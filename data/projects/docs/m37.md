# Own SPICE-like DC and transient solver

**Goal.** A circuit simulator built on modified nodal analysis (MNA) that parses a SPICE-style netlist, solves DC operating points with Newton-Raphson and transients with an implicit integrator, and agrees with ngspice on the circuits of project 36.

**Why.** Skills: modified nodal analysis, Newton-Raphson on nonlinear systems, and stiff integration (backward Euler, trapezoidal, local truncation error control). It joins the circuits of `mit-6-002` to the numerical methods of `mit-18-085` and `mit-2-086`, and it is the same stamp-assemble-solve pattern your FEM solver (`m50`) will use.

**Scope.** MVP: netlist parser for R, C, L, independent V and I sources (DC, pulse, sine), diodes (Shockley with series resistance) and a level-1 MOSFET; `.op` and `.tran` analyses; dense LU first, then sparse; CSV output and a plot viewer. Stretch: BJT (Ebers-Moll), `.ac` small-signal analysis, controlled sources, sparse LU with a fill-reducing ordering.

**Architecture.** The parser builds a list of elements and a node map. Each element knows how to stamp its conductances and currents into the MNA matrix; nonlinear ones stamp their linearised companion model at the current guess. The Newton loop restamps, solves and checks convergence. The transient driver replaces C and L with companion models for the chosen integrator, advances time, and adapts the step from the truncation-error estimate.

```
netlist ─▶ parser ─▶ elements ─▶ stamp (G, b) ─▶ LU solve ─▶ converged? ─no─┐
                                    ▲                            │yes        │
                                    └──────── Newton update ◀────┼───────────┘
                                                                 ▼
                                   transient driver (BE / trap, adaptive dt) ─▶ waveforms ─▶ viewer
```

**Stack.** C++20 or Rust, Eigen or your own LU, ngspice as the reference, Python only for the comparison script, and your own renderer from Stage 1 for the plot viewer. Nothing to buy.

**Milestones.** Netlist parser and linear MNA DC with R, V and I sources (7 h) · Newton-Raphson with diodes and the MOSFET, voltage limiting and gmin stepping (9 h) · transient analysis with backward Euler, trapezoidal and adaptive steps (10 h) · comparison harness against ngspice on the project 36 circuits (8 h) · plot viewer in your renderer and write-up (6 h). Total 40 h.

**Done when.** A 100-resistor ladder matches the analytical answer to 1e-12 relative. DC operating points of every project 36 circuit agree with ngspice within 1 mV or 0.1 %, whichever is larger. Transient waveforms (RC step, RLC ringing, rectifier, 555 astable) agree with ngspice within 1 % RMS of the signal swing, and the 555 frequency within 0.5 %. Newton converges on every test circuit, with the iteration count logged. Unit tests cover each element's stamp.

**Measure and report.** DC and transient error against ngspice per circuit; Newton iterations per time step; accepted and rejected steps under adaptive control; run time against ngspice for a 1 000-node RC mesh, dense against sparse; the ringing on an LC circuit with trapezoidal integration and how you damped it.

**Pitfalls.** Diode exponentials that overflow on the first Newton step without voltage limiting. Floating nodes that make the matrix singular (add gmin, or report them). Trapezoidal ringing on discontinuous sources. Sign conventions for current through voltage sources. Comparing against ngspice runs that used different tolerances (`reltol`, `abstol`) from yours.

**References.** mit-6-002, ngspice, mit-18-085, mit-2-086, art-of-electronics, m36.

**Write-up.** Overlays of your solver against ngspice for four circuits, the error table, a convergence plot for one Newton solve, and a paragraph on what `.ac` analysis would add.
