# CS336 assignments 1-2: BPE tokenizer, transformer and a training run

**Goal.** A byte-level BPE tokenizer trained on your corpus, a transformer language model assembled from components you wrote, and a logged training run with samples, passing the public CS336 assignment tests.

**Why.** CS336 asks for everything above raw tensor operations to be yours. It covers `inference-engineering/transformer-blocks`, `inference-engineering/attention-mechanism` and `ai-agents/tokenization`, and builds tokenization, transformer internals and profiling. Assignment 2 adds the systems view: where time and memory go in one training step.

**Scope.** MVP: BPE training with the GPT-2 pre-tokenization regex and special tokens, encode and decode, and a streaming encoder for large files; Linear, Embedding, RMSNorm, SwiGLU, RoPE, scaled dot-product attention, causal multi-head attention and the pre-norm transformer LM; cross-entropy, AdamW, a cosine schedule with warm-up, gradient clipping and checkpointing; a run on TinyStories; Assignment 2's benchmarking and profiling of the forward and backward passes, and its fused attention kernel as far as you take it. Stretch: the OpenWebText leaderboard run, distributed data parallel, optimizer state sharding.

**Architecture.** The tokenizer counts pre-tokens, learns merges from byte-pair counts and saves the vocabulary and merges; the encoder applies merges in rank order. Training data is tokenized once into a memmap. The model composes your modules; the loop samples batches and runs forward, loss, backward, clipping, the optimizer step and the schedule, logging to Weights & Biases. A profiling script times each component with CUDA synchronisation and records memory.

```
corpus ─▶ pre-tokenize ─▶ BPE merges ─▶ vocab, merges
corpus ─▶ encode ─▶ tokens (memmap)
batch ─▶ Embedding ─▶ [RMSNorm ─▶ MHA+RoPE ─▶ + ─▶ RMSNorm ─▶ SwiGLU ─▶ +] × L ─▶ logits
                                         loss ─▶ AdamW ─▶ checkpoints, W&B
```

**Stack.** Python 3.12, PyTorch 2.x with einops, uv as the course sets up, pytest with the assignment tests, Weights & Biases, the PyTorch profiler or Nsight Systems, one GPU (rented H100 hours or a consumer card).

**Milestones.** BPE training, encode and decode passing the tokenizer tests (10 h) · model components and the transformer LM passing their tests (10 h) · loss, optimizer, schedule, data loader and checkpointing (6 h) · TinyStories training run with a learning-rate sweep and samples (8 h) · Assignment 2 benchmarking and profiling (6 h). Total 40 h.

**Done when.** Every public test in Assignment 1 passes, and the Assignment 2 tests for the parts you did. The tokenizer matches the reference implementation's token ids on the test fixtures. The TinyStories run reaches the validation loss the handout sets for its compute budget (about 1.45 in the 2025 offering), with loss curves logged and generated samples in the README. No high-level model classes (`nn.Transformer`, `nn.MultiheadAttention`, Hugging Face models) are used.

**Measure and report.** Tokenizer compression in bytes per token, and training time, for 10k and 32k vocabularies; validation loss curves for at least three learning rates; tokens per second and peak memory; forward and backward time per component from the profiler; the effect of mixed precision on speed and loss.

**Pitfalls.** BPE training that recounts every pair after each merge and takes hours; update the counts incrementally. Merges that cross pre-token boundaries or special tokens. RoPE applied with the wrong pairing of dimensions. Timing GPU code without `torch.cuda.synchronize()`. Choosing the learning rate by training loss instead of validation loss.

**References.** stanford-cs336, paper-attention, paper-gpt2, w-ml-tokenizer-bpe, karpathy-zero-to-hero, wandb.

**Write-up.** The learning-rate sweep chart, samples at three checkpoints, the tokenizer compression table, the profiler breakdown of one training step, and what you would change for a larger run.
