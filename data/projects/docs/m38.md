# Audio amplifier or power supply on a self-designed KiCad PCB

**Goal.** Your first board, designed in KiCad, fabricated, assembled by hand, brought up in stages and measured against targets you wrote down first: either a 20–40 W audio amplifier or an adjustable bench power supply.

**Why.** Skills: KiCad schematic capture and PCB layout, soldering (through-hole and 0805 surface-mount), and staged bring-up. It is the step from breadboard to product, and every later board (`m46`, `m47`, the final capstone) reuses the habits formed here. Resources: `phils-lab`, `kicad`, `art-of-electronics`.

**Scope.** MVP, pick one. Amplifier: an LM3886 class-AB or TPA3116 class-D stage with input filter, power supply decoupling and output protection. Supply: a 0–20 V, 1 A linear supply (LM317 class or op-amp and pass transistor) with adjustable current limit. Either way: a design-targets table, a clean DRC, Gerbers, drill files, a BOM and a position file, test points on every rail. Stretch: a second revision fixing everything the first one taught you.

**Architecture.** Stages in order of bring-up: input power and protection (fuse, reverse-polarity), regulation and decoupling, the core stage (amplifier IC or pass element with its control loop), output and protection, then test points. Layout keeps the high-current loop small and the feedback node away from it.

```
DC in ─▶ fuse + reverse protection ─▶ rails + decoupling ─▶ core stage ─▶ output ─▶ load
                                          │                    ▲
                                          └── test points      └── feedback
```

**Stack.** KiCad 8, LTspice for the design check, a JLCPCB or PCBWay-class prototype service, the project 36 bench, Python for logging and plots, a PC sound card and REW or a scope FFT for distortion.

**Parts and budget.**
- PCB fabrication, five two-layer boards with shipping ($10–40)
- Components from the BOM ($15–40)
- Temperature-controlled soldering iron with a fine tip ($25–90)
- Solder, flux, tweezers, solder wick ($20–35)
- Hot-air station, optional for surface-mount rework ($0–80)
- Dummy load (power resistors) or a test speaker ($10–30)
- Heat sink and thermal pad ($5–15)
- Total: about $85–330

**Milestones.** Design targets, schematic and SPICE check (8 h) · layout, DRC and fabrication outputs, order placed (10 h) · assembly (6 h) · staged bring-up with a current-limited supply (6 h) · measurement against targets and write-up (10 h). Total 40 h.

**Done when.** The KiCad project passes ERC and DRC with zero errors and ships Gerbers, drill, BOM and position files. The board was powered stage by stage at current limit without a smoke event (or the event is in the README). Amplifier: gain within ±0.5 dB of design, THD+N under 0.1 % at 1 W and 1 kHz into 8 Ω, −3 dB points below 20 Hz and above 20 kHz. Supply: load regulation under 1 % from no load to 1 A, ripple under 10 mV peak-to-peak at 1 A, current limit within 10 % of the set value.

**Measure and report.** Amplifier: gain, frequency response, THD+N against output power, output noise in µV, heat-sink temperature at full power. Supply: line and load regulation, ripple in mV, transient response to a 0.1 A to 1 A load step, efficiency. In both cases, each measured number next to its target.

**Pitfalls.** Footprints that do not match the purchased part (check pinouts against datasheets before ordering). Missing thermal relief or undersized copper for the high-current path. Powering the whole board at once and losing the evidence of which stage failed. Ground layout that puts the speaker or load return through the input ground. Forgetting the heat sink until the thermal shutdown trips.

**References.** kicad, phils-lab, art-of-electronics, learning-the-art-of-electronics, ltspice, cu-boulder-power-electronics, m36.

**Write-up.** Board renders and photos of the assembled board, the bring-up log, the targets-against-measured table, one frequency-response or regulation plot, and the list of changes for revision B.
