# The same arm, built and driven with ROS 2

**Goal.** The two-link arm of project 54, built from printed links and geared motors, described in URDF, driven through ros2_control by a hardware interface you wrote, executing the trajectories planned in simulation, with tracking error measured and the real arm shown in RViz next to its digital twin.

**Why.** Skills: ROS 2 (nodes, launch files, parameters), ros2_control (controller manager, hardware interfaces, the joint trajectory controller), URDF, and hardware interfaces (a serial protocol between ROS and a microcontroller). It is the sim-to-real step for `m54` and rehearses the final capstone. Resources: `ros2-tutorials`, `articulated-robotics`, `modern-robotics`.

**Scope.** MVP: printed links and base sized from `m54`'s torque table; two geared motors with encoders and a position-velocity loop on a microcontroller; a binary serial protocol (commands down, joint states up, with checksums); a URDF matching `m54`'s parameters; a ros2_control `SystemInterface` plugin; the joint trajectory controller running the simulated trajectories; RViz with the real arm and the twin. Stretch: a gripper, gravity feed-forward from `m54`'s model, MoveIt 2 for Cartesian goals.

**Architecture.** The trajectory controller in the controller manager calls your hardware interface's `read()` and `write()` at 200 Hz. The interface talks to the microcontroller over USB serial; the microcontroller closes the joint loops at 1 kHz. The same URDF drives RViz and the twin in your engine, which plays the commanded trajectory in parallel.

```
trajectory ─▶ joint_trajectory_controller ─▶ hardware interface ─USB─▶ MCU (1 kHz loops) ─▶ motors
                       ▲                            │                        │
                       └──── /joint_states ◀────────┘◀────── encoders ◀───────┘
RViz: real arm ◀── /joint_states        twin (engine) ◀── same trajectory
```

**Stack.** ROS 2 Jazzy, ros2_control and ros2_controllers, C++ for the hardware interface plugin, C on the microcontroller, xacro and URDF, RViz, the project 54 Python models.

**Parts and budget.**
- Two geared DC motors with encoders ($40–70), or two smart servos such as Dynamixel XL430 ($100–120)
- Motor drivers for DC motors ($10–25)
- Microcontroller board, reused or new ($0–25)
- Filament for links and base ($5–10)
- Bearings, shafts and fasteners ($10–20)
- 12 V power supply ($10–20)
- Total: about $75–220

**Milestones.** Mechanical build of links, joints and base (8 h) · microcontroller joint loops and serial protocol (8 h) · URDF and ros2_control hardware interface (10 h) · simulated trajectories on the hardware with tracking error measured (8 h) · RViz with the digital twin and write-up (6 h). Total 40 h.

**Done when.** The URDF's lengths, masses and joint limits match `m54`'s parameter file (generated from it, ideally). The hardware interface runs at 200 Hz with under 1 % missed cycles over 10 minutes. The trajectories from `m54` run on the hardware with RMS joint tracking error under 2° and peak error under 5°. RViz shows the real arm and the twin side by side during a run, and the README includes the video.

**Measure and report.** RMS and peak tracking error per joint for each trajectory, against the simulated error; round-trip latency from `write()` to the next state in `read()`; loop timing jitter on the microcontroller; the difference between commanded and measured torque (from current) against `m54`'s predicted torques.

**Pitfalls.** Encoder counts per joint revolution wrong by the gear ratio. Serial framing without checksums, so a dropped byte becomes a wild command. Joint zero positions not homed consistently, so the twin and the arm disagree by a constant offset. A controller manager update rate the serial link cannot sustain. Gearbox backlash that the simulation does not include.

**References.** ros2-tutorials, articulated-robotics, modern-robotics, m54, m44, m-sim-arm-ros.

**Write-up.** A video of the arm and the twin moving together, the tracking-error table and plot, the hardware interface's design in a diagram, and a paragraph on what gravity feed-forward changed or would change.
