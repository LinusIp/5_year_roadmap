# 1D and 2D FEM solver, validated against an open solver

**Goal.** A finite-element solver written from the weak form up: 1D bar and Euler-Bernoulli beam elements that reproduce analytical solutions, then a 2D plane-stress solver with mesh import, sparse assembly and a linear solve, validated on the plate-with-hole benchmark against CalculiX or FEniCS and against the analytical stress concentration.

**Why.** Skills: weak forms and Galerkin discretisation, element assembly (shape functions, quadrature, stiffness matrices), sparse linear algebra, and verification (patch tests, convergence rates). It turns `m49`'s beam theory into a general method and uses the same stamp-and-solve pattern as `m37`. Resources: `fish-belytschko-fem`, `bangerth-dealii-lectures`, `mit-18-085`.

**Scope.** MVP: linear bar elements; two-node Hermite beam elements; 2D three-node triangles (CST) and four-node quads (Q4) for plane stress; Dirichlet and Neumann boundary conditions; mesh import from Gmsh; sparse assembly in coordinate format converted to compressed rows; a direct or conjugate-gradient solve; von Mises stress recovery; a convergence study. Stretch: six-node quadratic triangles, steady heat conduction in the same code (used by `m53`), stress plots in your own renderer.

**Architecture.** A mesh reader produces nodes, elements and boundary tags. For each element, shape functions and Gauss quadrature give a local stiffness matrix and load vector, scattered into a global sparse matrix. Boundary conditions are applied, the system is solved, and a post-processor recovers strains and stresses at Gauss points and averages them to nodes.

```
Gmsh mesh ─▶ reader ─▶ elements ─▶ local K_e, f_e ─▶ sparse assembly ─▶ BCs ─▶ solve
                                                                               │
                         VTK / plots ◀── stress recovery ◀── displacements ◀───┘
```

**Stack.** C++20 with Eigen (or Python with SciPy sparse), Gmsh for meshing, CalculiX or FEniCS for the reference, ParaView for viewing, pytest or GoogleTest. Nothing to buy.

**Milestones.** Weak form and 1D bar elements against analytical solutions (5 h) · Euler-Bernoulli beam elements (5 h) · 2D plane-stress triangles and quads, Gmsh import and sparse assembly (12 h) · patch test and plate-with-hole against CalculiX or FEniCS and the analytical concentration (10 h) · convergence study and write-up (8 h). Total 40 h.

**Done when.** Bar and beam elements reproduce analytical nodal displacements to 1e-10 for point and uniform loads, and match the ten `m49` problems. Both 2D elements pass the constant-strain patch test to machine precision. On a plate with a central hole under uniaxial tension, the peak hoop stress converges to within 2 % of CalculiX or FEniCS on the same mesh and within 3 % of the analytical concentration (Kirsch's factor of 3 for a wide plate, or a finite-width correction). Displacement error in the energy norm converges at the expected rate for linear elements, shown on a log-log plot.

**Measure and report.** Peak stress against element count for both element types; observed convergence rate against the theoretical one; difference from CalculiX or FEniCS at each refinement; assembly and solve time against degrees of freedom up to 10⁵; memory of the sparse matrix.

**Pitfalls.** Element node ordering that gives negative Jacobians. Plane-stress and plane-strain constitutive matrices swapped. Shear locking in Q4 elements under bending, mistaken for a bug or ignored. Stresses compared at nodes after averaging against a reference that reports Gauss-point values. Applying the traction as a point load at one node.

**References.** fish-belytschko-fem, bangerth-dealii-lectures, dealii-tutorials, calculix, fenics, mit-18-085, mit-2-001, m49.

**Write-up.** The plate-with-hole stress field, the convergence plot, the comparison table against the open solver and the analytical factor, and a paragraph on what quadratic elements would change.
