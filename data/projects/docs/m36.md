# Lab bench setup and ten classic circuits

**Goal.** A working electronics bench and ten textbook circuits, each predicted in LTspice or ngspice, built on a breadboard, measured, and plotted against its simulation with every disagreement explained.

**Why.** Skills: measurement technique (probe compensation, loading, grounding), SPICE, and reading datasheets. Every later Stage 2 project depends on trusting your instruments, and this is where you learn what a 10× probe, a 1 MΩ input and op-amp gain-bandwidth do to a reading. Resources: `learning-the-art-of-electronics`, `mit-6-002`, `ltspice`.

**Scope.** MVP: the bench, then ten circuits: voltage divider, RC low-pass and high-pass, series RLC, full-wave diode rectifier with smoothing, BJT and MOSFET low-side switches, common-emitter amplifier, op-amp inverting and non-inverting stages, comparator with hysteresis, 555 astable. Stretch: Bode plots swept automatically by scripting the scope and generator, a Monte Carlo SPICE run with component tolerances.

**Architecture.** One loop per circuit: datasheet values into a schematic, a SPICE prediction, a breadboard build, a scope capture exported as CSV, and a Python script that overlays both on the same axes and computes the error in the key figure.

```
datasheet ─▶ schematic ─▶ SPICE ──────────┐
                │                         ▼
                └─▶ breadboard ─▶ scope CSV ─▶ overlay + error ─▶ notes
```

**Stack.** LTspice or ngspice, KiCad's schematic editor for clean drawings, Python with NumPy and Matplotlib, the scope vendor's export tool or `pyvisa`.

**Parts and budget.**
- Bench power supply, 0–30 V, 3–5 A, with current limit ($50–90)
- Digital multimeter with capacitance and frequency ranges ($25–60)
- Oscilloscope: 2-channel USB scope or an entry bench scope ($80–400)
- Function generator, or $0 if the scope has one built in ($0–90)
- Breadboards and jumper wire ($15–25)
- Resistor, capacitor and inductor kits ($25–40)
- Semiconductors: 1N4007, 1N4148, 2N3904, 2N7000, LM358, TL072, NE555 ($15–25)
- Total: about $210–730

**Milestones.** Bench setup, probe compensation and instrument cross-checks (5 h) · divider, RC filters and RLC (6 h) · rectifier, BJT and MOSFET switches, common-emitter amplifier (9 h) · op-amp stages, comparator with hysteresis and 555 astable (9 h) · overlays, error table and write-up (6 h). Total 35 h.

**Done when.** The multimeter and scope agree on a DC rail within 1 % and the probes show a flat square wave. All ten circuits have a schematic, a simulated and a measured plot on the same axes, and an explanation. Key figures land inside stated bounds or are explained: RC corner frequency within 5 % of prediction, RLC resonance within 5 %, common-emitter gain within 15 %, comparator thresholds within 50 mV, 555 frequency within 10 %. Every error over 5 % names its cause (component tolerance, probe loading, slew rate, gain-bandwidth, breadboard capacitance).

**Measure and report.** A table of predicted, measured and error for one key figure per circuit; rectifier ripple in mV at a stated load; amplifier gain and −3 dB bandwidth; comparator hysteresis width; 555 frequency and duty cycle; the measured value of every resistor and capacitor you used against its nominal.

**Pitfalls.** An uncompensated or 1× probe that rounds every edge. Ground loops through the scope and the supply. Simulating with ideal parts while the breadboard has 2–5 pF between rows. Using an LM358 above a few kilohertz and blaming the circuit. Forgetting that most cheap generators expect a 50 Ω load and show double the amplitude into a high impedance.

**References.** learning-the-art-of-electronics, art-of-electronics, mit-6-002, ltspice, ngspice, cert-mitx-6002x.

**Write-up.** A photo of the bench, one overlay figure per circuit, the error table, and a short list of the measurement lessons you will carry into project 37's solver comparison.
