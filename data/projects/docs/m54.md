# Two-DOF robot arm: kinematics, dynamics, simulation

**Goal.** A planar two-link arm worked out on paper and in code before any hardware: forward and inverse kinematics and the Jacobian checked against the Modern Robotics library, equations of motion derived from Lagrange in SymPy, and trajectory tracking with computed-torque or PD-with-gravity control in your own engine.

**Why.** Skills: kinematics (product of exponentials or DH, the Jacobian, singularities), Lagrangian dynamics (mass matrix, Coriolis and gravity terms), and trajectory control. It is the model that `m55` builds and the digital twin that `m56` learns from. Resources: `modern-robotics`, `mit-2-12`, `mit-6-832-underactuated`.

**Scope.** MVP: a planar 2R arm with link masses, inertias and joint friction; FK, closed-form IK (elbow up and down) and the Jacobian; M(q), C(q, q̇) and g(q) derived symbolically and generated as code; simulation in your engine and as an ODE in SciPy for cross-checking; quintic joint trajectories and a Cartesian straight line; PD with gravity compensation and computed-torque control. Stretch: a third joint and numerical IK, actuator models with torque limits, an operational-space controller.

**Architecture.** One parameter file (lengths, masses, inertias, friction) feeds both the symbolic derivation and the engine. SymPy produces M, C and g as generated functions. A trajectory generator produces q_d, q̇_d, q̈_d; the controller computes torques; the engine (or the ODE) integrates the arm.

```
params ─▶ SymPy (Lagrange) ─▶ M(q), C(q,q̇), g(q) ─┐
                                                   ▼
trajectory (q_d, q̇_d, q̈_d) ─▶ controller τ = M(q̈_d + K_d ė + K_p e) + C q̇ + g ─▶ engine ─▶ q, q̇
                                       ▲───────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Stack.** Python with SymPy, NumPy and SciPy; the `modern_robotics` Python package for checks; your C++ engine with its multibody joints; Matplotlib for plots. Nothing to buy.

**Milestones.** Forward and inverse kinematics and the Jacobian, checked against the library (8 h) · Lagrangian equations of motion in SymPy with code generation (8 h) · simulation in your engine with energy checks and an ODE cross-check (9 h) · trajectories with PD-plus-gravity and computed-torque control (9 h) · write-up (6 h). Total 40 h.

**Done when.** FK and the Jacobian match `modern_robotics` to 1e-9 on 1 000 random configurations, and IK round-trips to 1e-9 away from singularities. With zero torque and no friction, total energy drifts by less than 0.1 % over 60 s at a 1 ms step, and the engine and the SciPy ODE agree on joint angles within 0.1° over 10 s. Computed-torque control tracks a 2-second quintic trajectory with RMS joint error under 0.5°, and still under 2° with 10 % error in the link masses. PD-with-gravity holds any static pose with zero steady-state error.

**Measure and report.** Energy drift against step size for each integrator; engine-against-ODE trajectory error; RMS and peak tracking error for both controllers, with and without model mismatch; peak joint torques for the chosen trajectory, which size the motors of `m55`.

**Pitfalls.** Angle conventions that differ between your derivation and the Modern Robotics library. A mass matrix missing the link's own rotational inertia. Coriolis terms with a factor-of-two error that only shows at speed. Simulation step and controller rate tied together without meaning to be. Trajectories that pass through the fully stretched singularity.

**References.** modern-robotics, mit-2-12, mit-6-832-underactuated, mit-2-003, sympy-tutorial, cert-modern-robotics, a-multibody-dynamics.

**Write-up.** The derivation summarised, the energy-drift plot, the tracking plots for both controllers, the torque table that sizes the motors, and a paragraph on what the real arm will add that the model leaves out.
