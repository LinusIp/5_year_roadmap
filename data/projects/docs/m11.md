# nanoGPT reproduction on a small dataset

**Goal.** A GPT written by you from an empty file in PyTorch, trained on a corpus you chose, with a validation loss curve, samples at several checkpoints and one measured ablation.

**Why.** Typing every line of a transformer is the shortest path to understanding one. It builds transformers, PyTorch and training loops, and the nodes `inference-engineering/transformer-blocks`, `inference-engineering/attention-mechanism`, `ai-agents/tokenization`, `ai-agents/temperature` and `ai-agents/top-p`. The model and its sampler are what the later inference projects take apart.

**Scope.** MVP: a character-level or GPT-2 BPE tokenizer (tiktoken); token and position embeddings; causal multi-head self-attention; MLP; pre-norm residual blocks; weight tying; AdamW with warm-up and cosine decay; gradient clipping; checkpoints; a sampler with temperature and top-k or top-p; one ablation. Stretch: mixed precision and `torch.compile`, `scaled_dot_product_attention`, a KV cache in the sampler, a larger run on a bigger corpus.

**Architecture.** A data script tokenizes the corpus into a uint16 memmap with a train and validation split. The model is a stack of blocks (LayerNorm, attention, residual; LayerNorm, MLP, residual) between an embedding and a tied output head. The training loop samples random windows, steps the optimiser, estimates validation loss every N steps and writes checkpoints. The sampler loads a checkpoint and generates autoregressively.

```
corpus ─▶ tokenize ─▶ train.bin / val.bin
                         │ random windows
                         ▼
emb ─▶ [LN ─▶ attn ─▶ + ─▶ LN ─▶ MLP ─▶ +] × N ─▶ LN ─▶ logits ─▶ loss
                                        checkpoints ─▶ sample.py
```

**Stack.** Python 3.12, PyTorch 2.x, tiktoken, NumPy, Weights & Biases or TensorBoard, one consumer GPU (or Colab) for a few hours of training.

**Milestones.** Bigram baseline, data pipeline and tokenizer (5 h) · attention, blocks and the full model with shape tests (8 h) · training loop, schedule, checkpoints and evaluation (6 h) · main training run with samples at checkpoints (6 h) · ablation runs (6 h) · write-up (4 h). Total 35 h.

**Done when.** The model, the loop and the sampler are typed by you, and a test checks that position t cannot see t+1. The final model beats the bigram baseline's validation loss by at least 30 %; on Tiny Shakespeare at character level, a 6-layer, 6-head, 384-wide model reaches validation loss below 1.6. Samples from three or more checkpoints are in the README. One ablation (context length, depth or width) runs at three settings with everything else fixed.

**Measure and report.** Train and validation loss curves; final validation loss and perplexity against the bigram baseline; parameter count; tokens per second and peak GPU memory; the ablation table, with validation loss at equal tokens seen and at equal wall-clock time.

**Pitfalls.** A causal mask that leaks the future, giving suspiciously low loss and garbage samples. Forgetting `model.eval()` and `torch.no_grad()` when estimating validation loss. Too few validation batches, so the curve is noise. No learning-rate warm-up, so training diverges in the first hundred steps. Comparing ablations at different token counts.

**References.** karpathy-zero-to-hero, paper-attention, paper-gpt2, stanford-cs336, w-ml-attention-numpy.

**Write-up.** The loss curves, samples from early, middle and final checkpoints side by side, the ablation table with a chart, and one paragraph on what the ablation taught you.
