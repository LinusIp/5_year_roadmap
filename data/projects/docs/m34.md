# XPBD cloth and soft bodies

**Goal.** Position-based dynamics in your 3D engine: cloth with stretch and bend constraints that drapes over rigid bodies, volume-preserving tetrahedral soft bodies, and a demonstration that stiffness does not depend on iteration count or time step, which is the point of XPBD.

**Why.** Game Developer roadmap: `game-developer/dynamics`, `game-developer/collision-detection` and `game-developer/ccd`. Skills: XPBD, constraint design, and GPU or multithreaded solvers. Position-based methods are what games and interactive tools use for deformables, and XPBD fixes PBD's main flaw: material stiffness that changes when you change the solver settings. The soft bodies and cloth join the rigid world of project 55 in the stage 1 capstone engine.

**Scope.** MVP: an XPBD core with substepping and per-constraint compliance; cloth from a grid mesh with distance constraints for stretch and dihedral or isometric bending constraints, pinned corners and a wind force; tetrahedral soft bodies with edge and volume constraints, meshes generated with TetGen or fTetWild; collision of particles against the rigid bodies' shapes with friction; self-collision for cloth attempted with a spatial hash and its limits documented; a parallel solver by graph colouring or Jacobi iterations. Stretch: two-way coupling where cloth pushes rigid bodies; tearing; a GPU solver in compute shaders.

**Architecture.** Each frame is split into substeps. A substep predicts particle positions from velocities and external forces, then projects each constraint with its compliance scaled by the substep squared, accumulating the Lagrange multiplier. Collision constraints are generated against the rigid world's shapes and, for cloth, against nearby particles from a spatial hash. Velocities are derived from the position change, with friction and damping applied last.

```
predict ─▶ constraints (stretch, bend, volume, collision) ─▶ project (compliance α/Δt²)
   ▲                                                              │
   └──────────── update velocities ◀── friction, damping ◀───────┘
rigid world (project 55) ─▶ collision shapes      spatial hash ─▶ self-collision
```

**Stack.** C++20 inside your 3D engine, your renderer for display, Dear ImGui to change compliance, iterations and substeps live, TetGen or fTetWild for tetrahedral meshes, OpenMP or a job system for the parallel solver.

**Milestones.** XPBD core: particles, substeps, compliance and constraint projection (6 h) · Cloth with stretch and bend constraints, pinned and draped (8 h) · Tetrahedral soft bodies with volume constraints and a meshing pipeline (9 h) · Collision against the rigid world, and a self-collision attempt with its limits documented (10 h) · Stiffness-independence demonstration, parallel solver and write-up (7 h). Total 40 h.

**Done when.** A 64×64 cloth drapes over a sphere and a box from your rigid engine without passing through, at 60 frames a second. A soft body dropped on the floor keeps its volume within 1 % of rest once settled. Stiffness independence is demonstrated: the sag of a hanging cloth and the stretch of a soft bar under load vary by less than 5 % across 5 to 50 iterations and time steps from 1/60 to 1/240 s, while plain PBD varies by far more on the same scenes. Self-collision's limits are written down with a video of where it fails.

**Measure and report.** Sag and stretch against iterations and time step for XPBD and PBD; volume error over time for soft bodies; solver time per frame against particle count with one thread and with all cores; penetration depth against the rigid world at rest; the frame rate for the largest cloth that stays interactive.

**Pitfalls.** Forgetting to reset the Lagrange multipliers each substep, which quietly turns XPBD back into PBD. Compliance not divided by the substep squared. Inverted tetrahedra from a large step, which the volume constraint then pushes the wrong way. Self-collision thickness smaller than the distance a particle travels per substep, so cloth tunnels through itself. Parallel Gauss-Seidel with races on shared particles.

**References.** paper-xpbd, paper-pbd, ten-minute-physics, paper-large-steps-cloth, nvidia-warp, m27.

**Write-up.** A video of cloth on rigid bodies and a squashing soft body, the stiffness-against-iterations chart for XPBD and PBD, the volume-error plot, the scaling table, and a section on self-collision: what works and where it breaks.
