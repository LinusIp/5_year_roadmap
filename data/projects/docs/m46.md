# Battery-powered device with a power budget and an enclosure

**Goal.** A small sensor logger that runs for weeks on one lithium cell: a power budget written before the design, a board with charging and protection, firmware that sleeps in microamps, a week-long measured test against the budget, and a printed enclosure you designed.

**Why.** Skills: low-power design (sleep modes, quiescent currents, duty cycling), battery management (charging, protection, capacity under load), and enclosure design (clearances, bosses, tolerances for printing). It is your second board after `m38` and the first that must survive unplugged. Resources: `making-embedded-systems`, `art-of-electronics`, `onshape-learning-center`.

**Scope.** MVP: an STM32L0/L4 or nRF52 that wakes on its RTC every 60 s, reads a temperature and humidity sensor, stores or transmits the reading, and sleeps; a LiPo or 18650 with a protection circuit; a charger IC with USB input; a regulator with under 5 µA quiescent current; a power budget spreadsheet; a printed enclosure with a window for the USB port. Stretch: fuel gauging from voltage and coulomb counting, radio transmission, an e-paper display.

**Architecture.** Power path: USB → charger → cell with protection → low-Iq regulator → MCU and sensor, with the sensor powered from a GPIO so it can be switched off. The firmware is a state machine: sleep, wake, power sensor, measure, store, power down, sleep. The budget models each state's current and duration.

```
USB 5 V ─▶ charger ─▶ cell + protection ─▶ low-Iq regulator ─▶ MCU ─GPIO─▶ sensor
                                                   │
                       sleep ─▶ wake (RTC) ─▶ measure ─▶ store ─▶ sleep
```

**Stack.** KiCad, C on your own drivers or the vendor low-layer library, a spreadsheet or Python notebook for the budget, Onshape or FreeCAD for the enclosure, a power profiler or a precision shunt with the scope.

**Parts and budget.**
- LiPo (1 000 mAh) or 18650 cell with protection ($5–12)
- Charger IC or module (MCP73831 or TP4056 class) ($1–5)
- Low-quiescent regulator (TPS7A02 or MCP1700 class) ($1–3)
- Microcontroller module with good sleep modes ($5–20)
- Temperature and humidity sensor (SHT40 class) ($3–8)
- Current measurement: a power profiler kit, or a precision shunt and amplifier ($15–110)
- PCB fabrication run and components ($10–40)
- Filament for the enclosure ($2–5)
- Total: about $42–203

**Milestones.** Requirements, power budget spreadsheet and part selection (5 h) · schematic, layout and order (9 h) · sleep-mode firmware and per-state current measurements (8 h) · enclosure CAD and print (5 h) · one-week battery test and write-up (8 h). Total 35 h.

**Done when.** The budget spreadsheet is committed before the board order, with predicted average current and battery life. Measured sleep current is under 10 µA (target 5 µA). The measured average current lands within 25 % of the budget, and the device runs for at least seven days on the cell with the voltage curve logged. The charger terminates at 4.2 V ±1 %, and the protection cuts out at under-voltage when tested with a bench supply in place of the cell. The enclosure closes over the board on the first print, or the README explains what was wrong.

**Measure and report.** Current and duration of each state against the budget; sleep current in µA; average current over a full cycle; predicted against extrapolated battery life in days; cell voltage over the test week; enclosure fit dimensions, nominal against measured.

**Pitfalls.** A floating GPIO or a debug probe left attached, adding hundreds of microamps. Pull-ups on I2C lines that leak through an unpowered sensor. Regulator quiescent current that dominates the whole budget. Treating the cell's rated capacity as usable at your temperature and load. Enclosure bosses that print undersized, so screws strip.

**References.** making-embedded-systems, art-of-electronics, phils-lab, kicad, onshape-learning-center, m38.

**Write-up.** The budget table with measured numbers beside predicted, a current-profile capture of one wake cycle, the week-long voltage plot, photos of the enclosure, and a paragraph on what radio transmission would cost in battery life.
