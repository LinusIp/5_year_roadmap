# xv6 labs write-up

**Goal.** A published series with one article per MIT 6.1810 lab you completed, each explaining what the lab asked, how xv6 does it, the key diffs you made and why, plus your own diagrams of xv6's address space and trap path, with no complete solutions posted.

**Why.** Explaining a kernel is the test of understanding it. It builds kernel internals and technical writing. No catalogue roadmap has operating-system nodes, so the anchors are resources: mit-6-1810 for the labs, ostep and cs162 for the concepts behind them.

**Scope.** MVP: articles for the labs you did (at least util, syscall, page tables, traps and copy-on-write fork; lock and file system if done), each with the problem, the xv6 mechanism, short annotated excerpts of your diffs, one bug story and a test transcript; the two diagrams. Stretch: an article on the scheduler and context switch, a gdb walkthrough of one system call from user space into the kernel and back, a short screencast.

**Architecture.** The repository holds one Markdown article per lab, the diagrams as editable sources and rendered images, and small code excerpts. A static site generator publishes them. The trap-path diagram follows one system call: `ecall` in user space, `uservec` in the trampoline, `usertrap`, `syscall`, the handler, `usertrapret`, `userret` and `sret`. The address-space diagram is checked against `memlayout.h`.

```
user: ecall ─▶ trampoline uservec ─▶ usertrap() ─▶ syscall() ─▶ sys_*()
          ▲                                                       │
          └─── userret (sret) ◀── usertrapret() ◀─────────────────┘
user VA: [text|data|guard|stack|heap … | trapframe | trampoline]
```

**Stack.** xv6-riscv, QEMU, the RISC-V GNU toolchain, gdb-multiarch, Markdown with a static site generator (Hugo, Astro or Jekyll on GitHub Pages), Excalidraw or Mermaid for diagrams.

**Milestones.** Outline, article template, util and syscall articles (6 h) · page-table article with the address-space diagram (6 h) · traps article with the trap-path diagram (6 h) · copy-on-write article (5 h) · remaining labs, editing pass and publishing (7 h). Total 30 h.

**Done when.** At least five articles are published, one per completed lab, each 800–2 000 words. Both diagrams are drawn by you and match the xv6 version you used. No article contains a complete solution: every excerpt is 30 lines or fewer and the rest is described in prose. Someone who has read OSTEP but not done the labs reads the traps article and can explain the trap path back to you.

**Measure and report.** Articles published and their word counts; the `make grade` result for each lab (reported, not the code); hours per lab against the course's estimate; feedback from at least two readers and what you changed because of it.

**Pitfalls.** A code dump instead of an explanation. Posting full solutions against the course's request. Diagrams that drift from the xv6 version you actually used. Leaving the writing to the end, when the details are gone; write each article within a week of its lab. Explaining what each line does and never why.

**References.** mit-6-1810, ostep, cs162, cmu-15-213.

**Write-up.** The series index with a one-line summary per article, both diagrams at the top, a "start here" article for new readers, and what you would study next (the same paths in Linux).
