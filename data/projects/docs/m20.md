# LSM-tree key-value store with a write-ahead log

**Goal.** An embedded LSM-tree storage engine with put, get, delete and range scan, a write-ahead log, sorted SSTables with Bloom filters, levelled or tiered compaction, and crash recovery that survives kill -9 at any point, with its write and read amplification measured and explained.

**Why.** LSM-trees power RocksDB, Cassandra and most write-heavy stores. This builds storage engines, crash consistency and benchmarking, and the nodes `software-architect/nosql-databases` and `software-architect/acid-cap-theorem`, making the durability in ACID concrete.

**Scope.** MVP: a memtable (skip list or ordered map); a WAL with per-record checksums and an fsync policy; flushes to immutable SSTables (data blocks, a sparse index, a Bloom filter, a footer); tombstones; a merging iterator for gets and scans; a manifest; background compaction, levelled or size-tiered; a crash-test harness. Stretch: a block cache, prefix compression, snapshots with sequence numbers, group commit for concurrent writers.

**Architecture.** A write appends to the WAL, then goes into the memtable. A full memtable becomes immutable and is flushed to a level-0 SSTable, after which its WAL segment is deleted. A read checks the memtable, the immutable memtables, then SSTables from newest to oldest, skipping any whose Bloom filter rules the key out. Compaction merges overlapping SSTables into the next level, dropping shadowed values and tombstones that reached the last level. The manifest records the live set of files and is replaced atomically.

```
put ─▶ WAL (fsync) ─▶ memtable ─▶ flush ─▶ L0 SSTables
                                               │ compaction
get ─▶ memtable ─▶ L0 ─▶ L1 ─▶ L2 …  ◀────────  L1, L2 sorted runs
       per SSTable: Bloom filter + index       manifest (atomic rename)
```

**Stack.** Rust, Go or C++ (pick one and stay with it), CRC32C, your own Bloom filter, a Python crash harness that sends SIGKILL at random points, a workload generator in the style of db_bench.

**Milestones.** Memtable and WAL with recovery (7 h) · SSTable format, flush and point reads (8 h) · Bloom filters, merging iterator and range scans (7 h) · compaction and manifest (9 h) · kill -9 crash tests (4 h) · amplification benchmarks and write-up (5 h). Total 40 h.

**Done when.** A randomised test of 1 million mixed operations agrees with an in-memory map. The crash harness kills the engine at 200 random points during writes, flushes and compactions; after every restart each acknowledged write is readable and no half-written record appears. With 10 bits per key, Bloom filters avoid at least 95 % of SSTable reads for absent keys. Write and read amplification are measured for at least one compaction setting and explained.

**Measure and report.** Write amplification (bytes written to disk over bytes written by the user), read amplification (SSTables or blocks read per get) and space amplification, for levelled and tiered settings; Bloom false-positive rate at 5 and 10 bits per key against theory; throughput and p99 latency for fill-random, read-random and scan workloads over 10 million keys.

**Pitfalls.** Fsyncing a new file but not its directory, so a fresh SSTable vanishes after a crash. Dropping tombstones before they reach the last level, so deleted keys come back. Treating a torn final WAL record as corruption instead of truncating it. Compaction that blocks all writes. Benchmarks small enough to live in the page cache, which measure memory, not disk.

**References.** ddia, paper-bigtable, w-sys-wal, w-bloom-filter, w-skip-list, cmu-15-445.

**Write-up.** A diagram of the levels before and after a compaction, the amplification table, the crash-test result, and a chart of throughput as compaction settings change.
