# Mixed-signal data-acquisition board

**Goal.** A data-acquisition board that measures the real world well: an analogue front end with gain and an anti-aliasing filter for a stated bandwidth, a 16-bit-or-better ADC over SPI, a mixed-signal layout you can defend, and host software that streams, records and plots. It becomes the instrument for the year 5 experiments.

**Why.** Skills: analogue front ends (instrumentation amplifiers, active filters, input protection), ADCs (reference, sampling, effective number of bits), mixed-signal layout (grounding, return paths, decoupling), and noise measurement. It reads the thermocouples of `m53` and logs every later rig. Resources: `art-of-electronics`, `learning-the-art-of-electronics`, `phils-lab`.

**Scope.** MVP: two differential channels, each with input protection, an instrumentation amplifier with selectable gain (1, 10, 100), a fourth-order Butterworth anti-alias filter at 1 kHz; a precision ADC sampling at 10 kSPS; a 2.5 V or 4.096 V reference; a microcontroller streaming over USB with sequence numbers; host software that plots live and records to CSV or Parquet. Stretch: four channels, a thermocouple cold-junction input, a calibration routine stored in flash.

**Architecture.** Signal path: connector → protection → instrumentation amp → anti-alias filter → ADC driver → ADC, referenced to a clean voltage reference. The ADC talks SPI to the microcontroller, which timestamps blocks and streams them over USB. Layout: one solid ground plane, analogue parts kept away from digital edges, decoupling at every pin.

```
sensor ─▶ protection ─▶ in-amp (G) ─▶ 4th-order LPF ─▶ ADC ◀─ Vref
                                                       │ SPI
                                         MCU ─USB─▶ host: stream ─▶ record ─▶ plot
```

**Stack.** LTspice for the front end, KiCad (four layers if the budget allows), C firmware with your SPI and USB drivers, Python with NumPy, SciPy and pyqtgraph for the host tool.

**Parts and budget.**
- Precision ADC, 16-bit or better (ADS1256, ADS131M04 or ADS8681 class) ($8–25)
- Instrumentation or low-noise op-amps (INA828, OPA2387 class) ($5–15)
- Voltage reference (REF5025 class) ($4–8)
- Microcontroller with USB (STM32 or RP2040 class) ($4–25)
- PCB fabrication run, two or four layers ($15–60)
- C0G capacitors and 0.1 % resistors for the filter ($10–20)
- Shielded cable and connectors (BNC or terminal blocks) ($10–30)
- Total: about $56–183

**Milestones.** Specification, front-end design and LTspice simulation (8 h) · schematic and mixed-signal layout (10 h) · assembly, bring-up and SPI and USB streaming firmware (9 h) · host software to stream, record and plot (6 h) · noise, ENOB and bandwidth characterisation and write-up (7 h). Total 40 h.

**Done when.** The front end's measured −3 dB point is within 10 % of 1 kHz and attenuation at 9 kHz is at least 60 dB. With inputs shorted, the noise floor is under 5 µV RMS referred to input at gain 100. ENOB is at least 14 bits measured from a clean sine, and gain error is under 0.1 % after a two-point calibration. The host streams for one hour at 10 kSPS on two channels with zero dropped sequence numbers. The README explains each layout rule you applied, with a screenshot.

**Measure and report.** Noise floor in µV RMS and noise spectral density for each gain; ENOB and SINAD at 100 Hz and 900 Hz; measured filter magnitude response against design; channel-to-channel crosstalk in dB; offset and gain drift over 30 minutes of warm-up; dropped packets and USB throughput.

**Pitfalls.** Digital return currents routed under the analogue front end. A reference without its required output capacitor that oscillates. Filter capacitors in X7R whose value changes with voltage and temperature (use C0G). Anti-alias corner set at Nyquist instead of well below it. Measuring noise with the USB cable carrying laptop charger noise into the ground.

**References.** art-of-electronics, learning-the-art-of-electronics, phils-lab, kicad, ltspice, dspguide, m38.

**Write-up.** Board photos and layout screenshots, the front-end schematic with the filter design, the noise histogram and FFT, the ENOB table, and a demo recording from a real sensor.
