# Heat-sink thermal model against thermocouple measurements

**Goal.** A heat sink whose temperature you predicted twice before measuring it, once with a lumped thermal-resistance network and once with your FEM solver, then measured with thermocouples read by your own data-acquisition board in natural and forced convection, with the predicted and measured curves overlaid.

**Why.** Skills: heat transfer (conduction, natural and forced convection, fin efficiency), thermal networks (resistances, capacitances, time constants), and experimental method (predictions committed first, calibration, uncertainty). It uses the DAQ board of `m47` and the solver of `m50`. Resources: `mit-2-006`, `mit-2-005`, `fish-belytschko-fem`.

**Scope.** MVP: a power resistor on an extruded heat sink dissipating about 10 W; a thermal network (junction or case to sink to ambient) with a lumped capacitance for the transient; a 2D steady conduction model of the fin cross-section in your FEM solver; three or four thermocouples (source, sink base, fin tip, ambient); natural convection and fan-forced runs; overlays. Stretch: a back-calculated convection coefficient against speed, an infrared camera image, a transient FEM run.

**Architecture.** The heater dissipates a measured electrical power (voltage times current). Heat flows through the paste into the base, along the fins, and into the air. The network predicts steady temperature rise and time constant; the FEM predicts the temperature field across a fin. Thermocouple amplifiers feed the DAQ board, which logs at 1 Hz to the host.

```
P = V·I ─▶ R_paste ─▶ R_base ─▶ R_fins ─▶ R_conv(h) ─▶ ambient
                         │          (C_sink)
thermocouples ─▶ MAX31855 ─SPI─▶ DAQ board (m47) ─USB─▶ host log ─▶ overlay with prediction
```

**Stack.** Python with NumPy and SciPy for the network and fits, your FEM solver extended to steady heat conduction, the project 47 board and host software, a bench supply for the heater.

**Parts and budget.**
- Extruded aluminium heat sink ($5–15)
- Power resistor (TO-220, 10–25 W) or a power transistor as the heat source ($3–8)
- Thermal paste ($5–8)
- Type-K thermocouples, four ($8–15)
- MAX31855-class thermocouple amplifiers, three or four ($10–45)
- Small 12 V fan, 40–80 mm ($5–10)
- Total: about $36–101, with the bench supply and project 47 board reused

**Milestones.** Thermal network, transient prediction and FEM model, committed before any test (8 h) · rig build, thermocouple calibration and DAQ integration (7 h) · natural-convection runs (5 h) · forced-convection runs (4 h) · overlays, discussion and write-up (6 h). Total 30 h.

**Done when.** Predictions are committed to the repository before the first test. Thermocouples agree within 1 °C at an ice bath and at room temperature. For natural and forced convection, the measured steady temperature rise of the sink base is within 20 % of prediction with the textbook convection coefficient, and the time constant within 25 %. The convection coefficient you had to assume is compared with the value that best fits the data, and the difference is discussed.

**Measure and report.** Heater power in W; steady temperature rise at each thermocouple; measured and predicted time constants; the effective thermal resistance sink-to-ambient in K/W for each case; the fitted convection coefficient in W/m²·K against correlation values; measurement uncertainty from thermocouple calibration and power.

**Pitfalls.** Thermocouple junctions not bonded to the surface, so they read the air. Radiation ignored in natural convection, where it can be a quarter of the loss. A test stopped before steady state (wait five time constants). Paste applied too thick, adding a resistance you did not model. Ambient drifting during an hour-long run without being logged.

**References.** mit-2-006, mit-2-005, fish-belytschko-fem, m47, m50.

**Write-up.** A photo of the rig, the network diagram with values, the predicted-against-measured overlays for both cases, the K/W table, and a paragraph on why the convection coefficient was the weakest assumption.
