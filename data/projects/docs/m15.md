# 2D rigid-body engine: SAT, impulses, joints and a sandbox

**Goal.** A 2D physics engine of your own, with convex polygons and circles, separating-axis collision, a sequential-impulse solver with warm starting, two joint types, and an interactive sandbox.

**Why.** Game Developer roadmap: `game-developer/collision-detection`, `game-developer/broad-phase`, `game-developer/narrow-phase`, `game-developer/sat`, `game-developer/sort-and-sweep`, `game-developer/joints`, `game-developer/friction`, `game-developer/restitution` and `game-developer/dynamics`. Skills: collision detection, constraint solving and numerical stability. This engine is the base of the year 2 capstone, the 3D engine and the learned simulator of project 52, so its correctness pays off three times.

**Scope.** MVP: circles and convex polygons, a fixed timestep, a sweep-and-prune or uniform-grid broadphase, SAT with clipping for contact manifolds, sequential impulses with friction, restitution and warm starting, distance and revolute joints, a sandbox to spawn and drag bodies. Stretch: sleeping, continuous collision for fast bodies, a prismatic joint, a deterministic replay file.

**Architecture.** Each step the broadphase produces candidate pairs. The narrowphase runs SAT on each pair and clips edges to build a manifold of up to two contact points, matched against last frame's contacts by feature id so accumulated impulses carry over. The solver pre-steps all contacts and joints, applies warm-start impulses, iterates velocity constraints, integrates positions, and the sandbox draws the world and its debug data.

```
bodies ─▶ broadphase ─▶ pairs ─▶ SAT + clip ─▶ manifolds
                                                  │
 joints ───────────────────────▶ impulse solver ◀─┘
                                       │
                         integrate ─▶ render / sandbox
```

**Stack.** C++20 (or the language of your year 1 game), raylib or SDL with Dear ImGui for the sandbox, doctest or Catch2 for unit tests.

**Milestones.** Bodies, semi-implicit Euler and a fixed timestep (5 h) · Broadphase and SAT narrowphase with contact manifolds (10 h) · Sequential impulses with friction, restitution and warm starting (10 h) · Distance and revolute joints (6 h) · Interactive sandbox with debug drawing (4 h) · Tests, measurements and the solver write-up (5 h). Total 40 h.

**Done when.** A stack of ten boxes stays standing for 60 seconds at 60 Hz with 10 solver iterations, the top box drifting less than 1 % of its width. A ball dropped with restitution 0.5 rebounds to 25 % of its height within 2 %. A box on a slope stays put below the friction angle and slides above it. A 20-link revolute chain hangs without visible stretch. Unit tests cover SAT on known overlapping and separated pairs, and the sandbox spawns, drags and deletes bodies.

**Measure and report.** Step time against body count from 100 to 2,000; broadphase pairs against true contacts; stack drift against solver iterations with and without warm starting; kinetic energy over time for a closed system with restitution 1.

**Pitfalls.** A variable timestep, which makes every stability result irreproducible. Contact normals that flip direction between frames, so warm starting pushes the wrong way. Applying restitution to resting contacts, which makes stacks jitter; use a velocity threshold. Clamping each impulse instead of the accumulated impulse, which loses the solver's convergence. Linear and angular units mixed in the inverse mass terms.

**References.** catto-box2d-publications, paper-iterative-dynamics, ericson-rtcd, millington-game-physics, gaffer-on-games, build-your-own-x.

**Write-up.** A sandbox GIF with stacking, a chain and a pile, the stability table against iterations, a step-time chart, and a page explaining the solver with the math of one contact constraint.
