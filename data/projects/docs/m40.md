# DSP: real-time FFT spectrum analyser on a microcontroller

**Goal.** A microcontroller that samples audio with a timer-triggered ADC and DMA, windows it, runs an FFT, and draws the spectrum on a small display at 20 frames per second or more, inside a hard real-time budget it can prove.

**Why.** Skills: sampling theory (Nyquist, aliasing, anti-alias filtering), DMA, fixed-point arithmetic (q15 against float), and real-time budgets. It is the embedded half of `a-dsp-visualiser` and builds on the drivers of `m39`. Resources: `dspguide`, `think-dsp`, `mit-6-003`.

**Scope.** MVP: a timer triggering the ADC at 32 kHz, circular DMA into a ping-pong buffer of two 512-sample halves, a 1 024-point Hann-windowed real FFT over the latest two halves (50 % overlap, a new frame every 16 ms), magnitude in dB, a log-frequency bar display, processing time shown on a GPIO pin. Stretch: q15 and float paths side by side, a spectrogram waterfall, peak-frequency readout with parabolic interpolation, an RC anti-alias filter in front of the ADC.

**Architecture.** The timer's trigger output starts each ADC conversion; DMA writes samples into a circular buffer and raises half-complete and complete interrupts. Each interrupt hands one half to the processing task, which windows, transforms, takes magnitudes and bins them into display columns. The display driver pushes the frame over SPI with DMA while the next half fills.

```
mic ─▶ preamp ─▶ ADC ◀─ timer trigger
                  │
                 DMA ─▶ [ half A | half B ] ─▶ window ─▶ FFT ─▶ |X| dB ─▶ bins ─▶ SPI DMA ─▶ LCD
                              ▲ half/full IRQ
```

**Stack.** C11 on the project 39 toolchain (or your own drivers plus CMSIS-DSP for the FFT), `arm-none-eabi-gcc`, OpenOCD, Python with NumPy for reference spectra, the bench function generator for test tones.

**Parts and budget.**
- STM32 board with an FPU, F4 or G4 family ($15–25, or $0 reusing project 39's)
- Electret microphone with MAX9814 preamp, or an analogue MEMS breakout ($5–12)
- SPI LCD (ST7789, 240×240) or I2C OLED (SSD1306) ($5–15)
- Total: about $10–55

**Milestones.** Timer-triggered ADC with circular DMA and ping-pong buffers (8 h) · dropped-sample proof with a test tone (4 h) · window, FFT and magnitudes, float and q15, timed on a GPIO pin (9 h) · display driver with SPI DMA at 20 FPS or more (8 h) · measurements and write-up (6 h). Total 35 h.

**Done when.** A 1 kHz test tone from the generator, captured for 10 minutes, shows no dropped samples: the DMA callback count matches the timer count, the overrun flag never sets, and a captured block has no phase jumps against an ideal sine. The tone appears in the expected bin (±1) and the measured frequency is within 0.1 % after interpolation. Window, FFT and binning finish in under 25 % of the 16 ms frame budget, measured on a scope from the GPIO pin. The display refreshes at 20 FPS or more, measured, not assumed.

**Measure and report.** Processing time per stage (window, FFT, magnitude, display) in µs for float and q15; CPU load; noise floor in dBFS with the input shorted; spurious-free dynamic range with a clean tone; difference between the on-device spectrum and NumPy's on the same captured block, in dB.

**Pitfalls.** Starting conversions from software instead of the timer, so the sample rate jitters. Processing a buffer half that DMA is still writing. Forgetting the window and blaming the FFT for leakage. q15 overflow in the butterflies without scaling. Spending the whole budget redrawing the display over polled SPI.

**References.** dspguide, think-dsp, mit-6-003, making-embedded-systems, phils-lab, cert-coursera-dsp, m39.

**Write-up.** A video of the analyser following a swept tone and a voice, the timing table with the GPIO scope capture, a NumPy-against-device spectrum overlay, and a paragraph on what a 4 096-point FFT would cost.
