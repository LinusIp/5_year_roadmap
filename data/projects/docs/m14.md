# CS:APP malloc, shell and proxy labs, written up

**Goal.** Two CS:APP labs done properly and explained: a dynamic memory allocator with an explicit or segregated free list, and a cache simulator with a cache-tuned matrix transpose, each with a design write-up that someone who has not done the lab can follow.

**Why.** These labs change how you see a program: memory becomes a heap you manage and a set of cache lines you hit or miss. They build memory layout, caches and performance measurement. No catalogue roadmap has nodes for this, so the anchors are the resources csapp-labs and cmu-15-213, with mit-6-172 for the measurement habits. The shell lab is covered by m07; the proxy lab is the stretch here.

**Scope.** MVP: the malloc lab (mm_init, malloc, free, realloc) with boundary tags, immediate coalescing, segregated free lists and a heap checker; the cache lab, part A (an LRU cache simulator driven by Valgrind traces) and part B (blocked transposes for 32×32, 64×64 and 61×67); two write-ups. Stretch: the proxy lab (a concurrent caching web proxy), and your allocator against glibc malloc on a real program.

**Architecture.** Allocator: the heap is a sequence of blocks with header and footer tags; free blocks also hold next and previous pointers into one of about a dozen size-class lists. malloc searches the right class, splits the block it finds, and extends the heap with sbrk when nothing fits; free coalesces with its neighbours and inserts the result. Cache simulator: parse each trace line, split the address into set index and tag, update the set's LRU order, count hits, misses and evictions.

```
malloc(n) ─▶ size class ─▶ seg list ─▶ fit ─▶ split ─▶ payload
free(p)   ─▶ coalesce(prev, next) ─▶ insert into seg list
heap: [prologue][hdr|payload|ftr][hdr|next,prev|…|ftr] … [epilogue]
trace ─▶ (set, tag) ─▶ LRU set ─▶ hit / miss / eviction
```

**Stack.** C, GCC, the lab handouts and their drivers (mdriver, test-csim, test-trans), gdb, Valgrind, perf.

**Milestones.** Implicit-list allocator passing the correctness traces (6 h) · explicit then segregated free lists with a heap checker (10 h) · cache simulator (5 h) · blocked transposes for the three sizes (6 h) · two write-ups with diagrams (8 h). Total 35 h.

**Done when.** mdriver runs every trace without errors and reports utilisation, throughput and the performance index, which you state (target 85 or more out of 100). test-csim matches the reference simulator on all traces. test-trans reports misses under the full-credit limits: under 300 for 32×32, under 1 300 for 64×64 and under 2 000 for 61×67. Each write-up explains every design choice with at least one diagram.

**Measure and report.** Utilisation and throughput per trace and the overall index for each allocator version (implicit, explicit, segregated); misses for each transpose size and blocking factor; a chart of utilisation against throughput as the design changed.

**Pitfalls.** Payloads not aligned to 8 or 16 bytes. Coalescing that forgets to unlink the neighbour from its free list. A heap checker written last instead of first. Conflict misses on the diagonal in the 64×64 transpose with naive 8×8 blocks. Publishing full lab solutions; the write-up explains, it does not post the code.

**References.** csapp-labs, cmu-15-213, ostep, mit-6-172.

**Write-up.** A heap diagram before and after a free with coalescing, the per-trace table, a chart of misses per blocking factor, and what you would change for a multithreaded allocator.
