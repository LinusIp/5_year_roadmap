# CAD: ten parts and two assemblies, printed and measured

**Goal.** Ten fully constrained parametric parts of rising difficulty, two assemblies with mates and motion, dimensioned drawings with tolerances a machinist could work from, and every part printed and measured with calipers against its nominal dimensions.

**Why.** Skills: parametric modelling (design intent, fully constrained sketches, configurations), engineering drawings (views, sections, dimensioning), tolerances (fits, general tolerances, what a printer can hold), and 3D printing. Every later Stage 2 build (`m51`, `m55`, `cap-final`) starts here. Resources: `onshape-learning-center`, `freecad-docs`, `mit-2-008`.

**Scope.** MVP: ten parts, from a bracket with holes to a part with drafts, fillets, a shell and a pattern (for example: spacer, L-bracket, motor mount, enclosure lid with snap fits, pulley, gear blank, hinge, clamp, bearing housing, handle with a swept profile); two assemblies with mates and a motion study (a hinge box and a small crank-slider); exploded views; drawings with a title block, general tolerances and toleranced fits on the critical features. Stretch: the same three parts scripted in CadQuery, a sheet-metal part, GD&T frames on one drawing.

**Architecture.** One pipeline per part: design intent, sketch with full constraints, features, drawing, slicer, print, measurement, and a correction fed back to the model. A shared spreadsheet holds nominal, measured and error for every critical dimension.

```
intent ─▶ sketch (fully constrained) ─▶ features ─▶ drawing
                                          │
                                          ▼
                     slicer ─▶ print ─▶ calipers ─▶ error table ─▶ model correction
```

**Stack.** Onshape (free plan) or FreeCAD, CadQuery for the stretch, PrusaSlicer or Cura, a spreadsheet or Python with pandas for the measurement table.

**Parts and budget.**
- 3D printer access: owned, a makerspace, or an entry FDM printer if you must buy ($0–350)
- Filament, 1 kg PLA or PETG ($18–25)
- Digital calipers, 0.01 mm resolution ($15–30)
- Total: about $33–55 with printer access, up to $405 with a new printer

**Milestones.** Parts one to five with full constraints (10 h) · parts six to ten (10 h) · two assemblies with mates, motion and exploded views (7 h) · drawings with tolerances for every part (6 h) · print, measure, correct the models and write up (7 h). Total 40 h.

**Done when.** Every sketch is fully constrained, and changing one driving dimension per part regenerates without errors (shown in a short screen capture). Both assemblies move through their range without interference. All ten parts have drawings with tolerances. All parts are printed and at least 30 critical dimensions measured; the table lists nominal, measured and error. After one round of model or slicer compensation, mean absolute error on reprinted features drops below 0.15 mm and holes fit their mating pins as designed.

**Measure and report.** Mean and worst dimensional error for outer dimensions, holes and slots, before and after compensation; hole shrinkage against diameter; the clearance you needed for sliding and press fits on your printer; print time and mass per part.

**Pitfalls.** Sketches that look right but are under-constrained, so they break when a dimension changes. Holes that print 0.2–0.4 mm undersized and surprise you at assembly. Measuring a part with elephant's foot at the base. Tolerances on the drawing tighter than the process can hold. Mating to faces that change topology when the model is edited.

**References.** onshape-learning-center, freecad-docs, cadquery-docs, mit-2-008, mit-2-007, cert-cswa.

**Write-up.** Renders and photos of all ten parts, the two drawings you are proudest of, the nominal-measured-error table, the before-and-after compensation chart, and the fit clearances you will use from now on.
