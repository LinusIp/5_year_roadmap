# FPGA: UART and a VGA or HDMI pattern generator

**Goal.** Your first real hardware description on an FPGA: a UART you can type into from a terminal, and a 640×480 video timing generator that draws test patterns and a sprite you move with keystrokes over that UART.

**Why.** Skills: Verilog or SystemVerilog, self-checking testbenches, and timing constraints (clocks, false paths, clock-domain crossing). It is the hardware twin of `harris-ddca` chapters 4–5 and the platform for the RISC-V core of `m43`. Resources: `nandland`, `getting-started-with-fpgas`, `project-f`.

**Scope.** MVP: a 115 200-baud 8N1 UART receiver (16× oversampling) and transmitter with a FIFO; a VGA or DVI/HDMI timing generator at 640×480 and 60 Hz from a 25.175 MHz (or 25 MHz) pixel clock; colour bars, a checkerboard and a gradient; a 32×32 sprite whose position is set by W, A, S, D bytes from the UART; a synchroniser between the UART and pixel clock domains. Stretch: TMDS encoding written yourself for HDMI, a text mode with a font ROM, 1280×720.

**Architecture.** The UART receiver turns serial bits into bytes and pushes them into a FIFO; a command decoder updates the sprite registers. The timing generator counts pixels and lines and emits hsync, vsync, blanking and the current (x, y). A pattern mixer picks the colour from the selected pattern and overlays the sprite. The output stage drives the VGA resistor DAC or the TMDS serialisers.

```
PC ─serial─▶ UART RX ─▶ FIFO ─▶ cmd decoder ─▶ sprite regs ─(CDC)─┐
                                                                  ▼
 pixel clock ─▶ timing gen ─(x, y, sync)─▶ pattern mixer ─▶ VGA DAC or TMDS ─▶ monitor
```

**Stack.** Verilog or SystemVerilog; Yosys, nextpnr and the open iCE40 or ECP5 flow, or Vivado for Artix-7; Icarus Verilog or Verilator with GTKWave for simulation; cocotb optional for Python testbenches; a serial terminal.

**Parts and budget.**
- FPGA board: iCEBreaker (iCE40UP5K) with a VGA or DVI PMOD ($70–95), or a Basys 3 (Artix-7, VGA on board) ($150–170)
- Monitor that accepts 640×480, and a VGA or HDMI cable ($0–20)
- USB-serial adapter if the board has no USB UART ($0–10)
- Total: about $70–200

**Milestones.** Toolchain, constraints file and a blinky (4 h) · UART transmitter and receiver with self-checking testbenches (8 h) · video timing generator and test patterns (8 h) · sprite moved over the UART, with clock-domain crossing (7 h) · timing closure, resource report, captures and write-up (8 h). Total 35 h.

**Done when.** The UART testbench sends 1 000 random bytes, including back-to-back frames and a ±2 % baud error, with zero mismatches, and the waveforms are in the README. On hardware, 1 MB echoed through the FPGA arrives without errors. The monitor locks to 640×480 at 60 Hz; hsync measures 31.47 kHz ±0.5 % and vsync 59.94 Hz on the scope. The sprite moves on every keystroke without tearing. Place and route reports zero timing violations with positive slack on every clock, and LUT, flip-flop and block-RAM use are reported.

**Measure and report.** Resource use per module; maximum frequency against the required pixel clock; worst negative and total slack; UART bit-error rate against baud mismatch in simulation; measured sync frequencies; build time for each toolchain step.

**Pitfalls.** Unregistered asynchronous inputs (the UART RX pin) that cause metastability. Front porch, sync and back porch in the wrong order, or the wrong sync polarity. Blocking assignments in clocked logic that simulate fine and synthesise differently. Inferred latches from incomplete `case` statements. No constraints file, so the tool reports success at an unknown frequency.

**References.** nandland, getting-started-with-fpgas, project-f, fpga4fun, harris-ddca, mit-6-004.

**Write-up.** A photo of the monitor with each pattern, a GIF of the sprite moving, the UART simulation waveform, the timing and resource tables, and a paragraph on what changes at 1280×720.
