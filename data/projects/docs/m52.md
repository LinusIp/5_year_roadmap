# 12 Steps to Navier-Stokes, then your own 2D CFD

**Goal.** Lorena Barba's twelve steps completed as notebooks, then an incompressible 2D Navier-Stokes solver rewritten in your own language that reproduces published lid-driven cavity and channel-flow data and shows velocity, vorticity and streamlines live in your own renderer.

**Why.** Skills: finite differences (upwind and central schemes, CFL and diffusion limits), pressure projection (the pressure Poisson equation, staggered grids), and validation against benchmarks. It is the engineering counterpart of the graphics fluids you built in Stage 1, now held to published numbers. Resources: `cfd-python-12-steps`, `mit-2-006`, `mit-2-086`.

**Scope.** MVP: all twelve steps (linear and nonlinear convection, diffusion, Burgers, Laplace, Poisson, cavity and channel flow) as notebooks; a C++ or Rust solver on a staggered (MAC) grid with a projection method; a pressure solver (conjugate gradient or multigrid); lid-driven cavity at Re 100 and 1 000; plane Poiseuille channel flow; live visualisation. Stretch: flow past a cylinder with vortex shedding and a measured Strouhal number, a GPU pressure solve.

**Architecture.** Each time step: advect and diffuse the velocity to an intermediate field, solve the pressure Poisson equation for the divergence of that field, subtract the pressure gradient to make it divergence-free, apply boundary conditions. A renderer reads the fields every frame and draws colour maps, vorticity and streamlines.

```
u^n ─▶ advect + diffuse ─▶ u* ─▶ ∇²p = ∇·u*/Δt ─▶ u^{n+1} = u* − Δt∇p ─▶ BCs
                                     (CG / multigrid)                      │
                         renderer: |u|, ω, streamlines ◀───────────────────┘
```

**Stack.** Python with NumPy and Jupyter for the twelve steps; C++20 or Rust for the solver; your own OpenGL or Vulkan renderer; published cavity data (Ghia, Ghia and Shin, 1982) as CSV for validation. Nothing to buy.

**Milestones.** The twelve steps as notebooks (14 h) · solver port with projection and a CG or multigrid pressure solve (10 h) · lid-driven cavity and channel-flow validation (7 h) · live visualisation in your renderer (5 h) · write-up (4 h). Total 40 h.

**Done when.** All twelve notebooks run top to bottom. On a 129 × 129 grid, centreline velocity profiles of the lid-driven cavity at Re 100 and Re 1 000 match the Ghia data with RMS error under 2 % of the lid velocity, and the primary vortex centre is within one cell. Channel flow converges to the analytical parabolic profile within 0.5 % of peak velocity. The renderer shows a 128 × 128 simulation live at 30 FPS or more.

**Measure and report.** Centreline profiles overlaid on the benchmark data; RMS error against grid size (33², 65², 129²) and the observed order of convergence; pressure-solver iterations per step and time per step; maximum divergence after projection; frames per second at each grid size.

**Pitfalls.** Time steps above the CFL or diffusion limit, so the solution blows up at Re 1 000. Pressure boundary conditions inconsistent with the velocity ones. Comparing collocated-grid values to the benchmark without interpolating to the same points. A pressure solve stopped too early, leaving divergence that looks like numerical viscosity. Running at a resolution too coarse to resolve the secondary corner vortices and calling it validated.

**References.** cfd-python-12-steps, mit-2-006, mit-2-086, mit-18-085, bridson-fluid-sim, ten-minute-physics.

**Write-up.** A video of the cavity spinning up in your renderer, the benchmark overlay plots, the grid-convergence table, and a paragraph on what flow past a cylinder would test that the cavity does not.
