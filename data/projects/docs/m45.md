# Inverted pendulum: simulate in your own engine, then build and control

**Goal.** A cart-pole modelled in your own multibody engine, stabilised there by an LQR designed on the linearised model, then built for real on a belt-driven cart and balanced for a minute through a tap, with simulated and measured trajectories overlaid.

**Why.** Skills: state-space control, LQR, sensor filtering (velocity from encoders, complementary or Kalman filters), and sim-to-real. It is the classic test of whether your model in `a-multibody-dynamics` predicts hardware, using the motor stage of `m44`. Resources: `mit-6-832-underactuated`, `brunton-control-bootcamp`, `feedback-systems`.

**Scope.** MVP: nonlinear cart-pole in your engine with friction and motor dynamics; linearisation about upright; LQR with Q and R chosen and justified; balance from a manual start near upright; limit switches and a software cart-travel limit. Stretch: energy-based swing-up, a Kalman filter for the velocity states, LQR gains scheduled from identified parameters.

**Architecture.** Two encoders give cart position and pendulum angle. A 500 Hz control loop estimates the four states (x, ẋ, θ, θ̇) by filtered differentiation, computes u = −Kx, converts the force command to a motor voltage through the identified motor model, and drives the bridge. A supervisor disables the motor at the travel limits or when the angle leaves ±25°. The same controller code runs against the engine through a thin interface.

```
θ encoder ─┐
           ├─▶ state estimate (x, ẋ, θ, θ̇) ─▶ u = −Kx ─▶ force→voltage ─▶ H-bridge ─▶ cart
x encoder ─┘                                                                      │
          ▲───────────────────── pendulum + cart (real or engine) ◀───────────────┘
```

**Stack.** Your engine for the simulation, Python with python-control for linearisation and `lqr`, C on the microcontroller for the loop, serial telemetry at 500 Hz, the motor and driver of project 44.

**Parts and budget.**
- Linear rail or belt-driven cart: a 3D-printer axis, or GT2 belt, pulleys, 8 mm rods and linear bearings ($25–60)
- Motor and driver from project 44 ($0, reused)
- Rotary encoder for the pendulum angle, 600 lines or more ($15–35)
- Two limit switches ($2–5)
- Pendulum rod, bearings and printed mounts ($10–20)
- Microcontroller board, reused or new ($0–25)
- 12–24 V supply sized for the motor, reused or new ($0–25)
- Total: about $50–170

**Milestones.** Nonlinear model and LQR working in your engine (8 h) · mechanical build, encoders and limit switches (9 h) · identification of cart friction, motor gain and pendulum period and damping (6 h) · real-time LQR at 500 Hz with state filtering and safety (10 h) · simulated against measured trajectories, model error and write-up (7 h). Total 40 h.

**Done when.** In simulation, LQR recovers from 15° initial angles and from a 0.5 N·s impulse. On hardware, the pendulum balances for at least 60 s with the cart staying within ±15 cm of centre, and recovers from a tap that tilts it by 5° or more within 2 s, five times out of five. Simulated and measured responses to the same tap are overlaid, with RMS angle error reported and its sources discussed.

**Measure and report.** Identified parameters with uncertainty; the LQR gains and closed-loop poles; RMS pendulum angle and cart excursion during 60 s of balancing; recovery time and peak cart travel after a tap; RMS error between simulated and measured θ(t) and x(t); loop timing jitter in µs.

**Pitfalls.** Belt stretch and backlash that the model does not include. Differentiated encoder angles too noisy for the θ̇ gain. Sign conventions that differ between the model, the encoder and the motor, so the controller pushes the wrong way. Coulomb friction on the cart that the linear model ignores, causing a limit cycle. No hard stop on cart travel before the first test.

**References.** mit-6-832-underactuated, brunton-control-bootcamp, feedback-systems, brian-douglas-control, mit-2-004, a-multibody-dynamics, m44.

**Write-up.** A video of balancing through taps, the sim-against-real overlay, the gain and pole table, and a paragraph on the largest source of model error and how swing-up would change the controller.
