# Statics and beam calculator, validated against hand calculations

**Goal.** A small Python tool that does what 2.001 teaches for beams: reactions, shear and moment diagrams, bending stresses, slopes and deflections for simply supported and cantilever beams under any mix of point, distributed and moment loads, checked against ten problems solved by hand.

**Why.** Skills: statics (free-body diagrams, equilibrium), beam theory (Euler-Bernoulli, flexure formula, superposition), and SymPy or NumPy. It is the hand-calculation baseline that your FEM solver (`m50`) must reproduce, and it practises the habit of checking every number twice. Resources: `mit-2-001`, `sympy-tutorial`, `scientific-python-lectures`.

**Scope.** MVP: simply supported and cantilever beams; point forces, point moments, uniform and linearly varying distributed loads; reactions from equilibrium; shear, moment, slope and deflection through singularity (Macaulay) functions in SymPy, evaluated numerically with NumPy; bending and shear stress for rectangular, circular and I-sections; plots of all four diagrams. Stretch: overhanging and propped-cantilever (statically indeterminate) beams, a command-line input format, a small web front end.

**Architecture.** A beam description (length, supports, section, material, loads) feeds a load model expressed as singularity functions. Integrating gives shear, moment, slope and deflection; boundary conditions fix the integration constants; a section module gives stresses. A checker compares any result with a hand-calculation table.

```
beam spec ─▶ loads as singularity functions ─▶ ∫ V ─▶ ∫ M ─▶ ∫ θ ─▶ ∫ v
                                                         ▲
                                         boundary conditions
                     ─▶ stresses (σ = My/I, τ = VQ/Ib) ─▶ plots + check table
```

**Stack.** Python 3.12, SymPy, NumPy, Matplotlib, pytest. Nothing to buy.

**Milestones.** Load model and reactions (5 h) · shear and moment through singularity functions (6 h) · slope, deflection and section stresses (6 h) · ten textbook problems by hand and by tool (5 h) · plots, tests and write-up (3 h). Total 25 h.

**Done when.** Ten textbook problems (at least three cantilevers, three simply supported, two with distributed loads, two combining three or more load types) are solved by hand on paper and by the tool, and the reactions, maximum moment, maximum stress and maximum deflection agree within 0.5 %. The symbolic and numeric paths agree to 1e-9. Each diagram is checked against equilibrium: shear jumps equal point loads, moment jumps equal applied moments, and the area under the shear diagram equals the change in moment. pytest covers every load type.

**Measure and report.** The side-by-side table of hand and tool results for the ten problems with percentage differences; the cases where the hand solution was wrong and how the tool found it; run time per problem.

**Pitfalls.** Sign conventions for shear and moment that differ between the textbook and your code. Macaulay brackets evaluated as ordinary powers for negative arguments. Integration constants solved with the wrong boundary conditions for a cantilever (slope and deflection zero at the wall, not at the free end). Units mixed between N·mm and N·m. Using the wrong second moment of area for the bending axis.

**References.** mit-2-001, sympy-tutorial, scientific-python-lectures, mit-2-086, cert-fe-exam.

**Write-up.** One worked problem shown three ways (hand, symbolic, plotted), the ten-problem comparison table, the four diagrams for the most complex beam, and a paragraph on what indeterminate beams add.
