# Gearbox or linkage mechanism: design, simulate, print, test

**Goal.** A mechanism with a job, either a two-stage spur reduction gearbox (about 20:1) or a four-bar linkage that traces a specified path, designed in CAD from a kinematic specification, checked in simulation for motion and loads, printed, assembled, measured, and loaded until one part fails.

**Why.** Skills: mechanism design (Grashof condition, transmission angle, gear ratios), gear geometry (module, pressure angle, involute profile, contact ratio), design for printing (orientation, clearances, layer direction under load), and testing to failure. It builds on the CAD and tolerance habits of `m48` and the stress analysis of `m49`. Resources: `mit-2-007`, `mit-2-008`, `mit-2-003`.

**Scope.** MVP, pick one. Gearbox: ratio, output torque and module specified first; involute spur gears generated in CAD; shafts on bearings; Lewis bending stress estimates per tooth. Linkage: a target coupler curve specified first, link lengths synthesised (graphically or by optimisation), pin forces from a quasi-static analysis. Both: motion simulated in your engine or the CAD package, printed in PETG or nylon, efficiency or positional error measured, one part loaded to failure. Stretch: a helical or planetary variant, an optimised linkage with minimum transmission angle above 40°.

**Architecture.** Specification drives the kinematic design; the CAD model is generated from it; simulation gives motion and loads; loads size the parts; the printed build is tested and the results go back into the specification.

```
spec (ratio or path) ─▶ kinematic design ─▶ CAD ─▶ motion sim ─▶ loads ─▶ sizing
         ▲                                                                 │
         └──────── test results ◀── measure ◀── assemble ◀── print ◀───────┘
```

**Stack.** Onshape or FreeCAD (with a gear generator), your engine or the CAD motion study, Python for synthesis and load calculations, PrusaSlicer or Cura, a camera for path tracking in the linkage case.

**Parts and budget.**
- Filament, PETG or nylon for gears ($20–40), on a printer you can use
- Bearings (608 or 625 class) ($5–10)
- Steel shafts or rod, 5–8 mm ($5–10)
- M3 and M4 fastener kit ($8–15)
- Small motor (N20 class, or the project 44 motor) ($0–20)
- Torque measurement: a lever arm and a luggage or kitchen scale ($10–20)
- Total: about $48–115

**Milestones.** Kinematic specification and synthesis or gear geometry (7 h) · CAD with printing tolerances (8 h) · motion simulation and tooth or pin load estimates (7 h) · print, assemble and iterate (7 h) · efficiency or positional-error tests and a failure test (7 h) · write-up (4 h). Total 40 h.

**Done when.** The ratio or coupler curve is written down before any CAD. The simulated motion matches the specification (ratio exact; coupler curve within 1 mm RMS). The printed gearbox turns its output once per designed number of input turns (counted over ten output turns) and its efficiency is measured at three loads; or the printed linkage traces its path with RMS error under 2 mm, measured from video. One part is loaded until it fails, the failure load is compared with the prediction (expect within ±40 % for prints, and say why), and the fracture surface is photographed and explained.

**Measure and report.** Efficiency against output torque, or path error along the curve; backlash in degrees at the output; predicted against actual failure load; the failure mode (tooth root, pin bearing, layer delamination); print time and mass.

**Pitfalls.** Gear clearance too tight for printed tolerances, so the train binds. Printing gears with layers parallel to the load, so teeth shear between layers. A linkage that passes through a toggle position the synthesis ignored. Measuring efficiency without subtracting the motor's own losses. Lewis estimates with the wrong form factor for low tooth counts.

**References.** mit-2-007, mit-2-008, mit-2-003, mit-2-001, onshape-learning-center, m48.

**Write-up.** The specification, a GIF of the simulated and the real mechanism, the efficiency or path-error chart, the failure photo with its explanation, and a paragraph on the next revision.
