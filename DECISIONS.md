## Architecture

- **No router dependency.** Routing is a ~100-line hash router (`src/router`). GitHub Pages serves static
  files, so `/roadmap` would 404 on reload while `#/roadmap` always works, offline too. React Router 8 was
  the alternative; it would be the only reason to carry a routing library.
- **Curriculum is a build-time virtual module.** `/data/**/*.yaml` is parsed and Zod-validated by a small
  Vite plugin (`scripts/lib/vite-plugin-seed.ts`) and served to the app as `virtual:atlas-seed`. A schema
  error or duplicate id fails `vite build`, `npm run build` and `npm run validate:data` alike. Nothing from
  the curriculum is written into IndexedDB, which is what makes "edit a YAML file, rebuild, keep every
  log" true by construction rather than by migration.
- **Seed ids must be unique across resources, projects, papers and certs**, not just within each file.
  `DayLog.entries[].refId` and `UserItemState.refId` carry no "kind", so a shared id would be ambiguous.
- **Plan item ids derive from what is planned** (`plan.<resource-or-project-id>`), not from position, so
  reordering `plan.yaml` or moving an item to another phase never orphans a user's plan edits.
- **Scripts run on Node's native TypeScript support** (type stripping, Node >= 22.18), so there is no `tsx`
  or `ts-node`. The cost: relative imports carry their `.ts`/`.tsx` extension everywhere, and the code
  avoids non-erasable syntax (`enum`, parameter properties); `erasableSyntaxOnly` enforces it.
- **Service worker is hand-written** (about 40 lines, generated by `scripts/lib/vite-plugin-sw.ts`) instead
  of Workbox/vite-plugin-pwa, which would add several hundred transitive packages. It precaches the build
  output, serves the app shell for navigations, ignores every cross-origin request, and is unit-tested
  against fakes of `self` and `caches`.
- **Stricter than the brief on links.** The schema has no "unverified URL" state at all: a link is either
  verified (`url`, `urlVerified: true`, `lastVerified`) or absent (`url: null`, `searchHint`). An invented
  URL cannot be committed by accident.
- **The authoritative data model is kept verbatim** in `src/seed/model.ts`; `tests/unit/model.test.ts`
  proves at compile time that every schema is a superset of it. Extensions are optional fields only.

## Design

- **The look is soft clay** (CLAY-STYLE.md, pull request #5): one background, raised tiles and knobs, sunken
  wells, blue and ice only. See "Soft clay" below; it replaces the earlier style rules of this file.
- **Colour never names a track or a block.** A block is told apart by its icon, in blue or ice; a track by its
  name. The heatmap is one ordered ramp (`--ramp-0` … `--ramp-4`), and a track filter keeps the same ramp and only
  counts that track's minutes. Charts take their series colours from the ramp, ice and blue, in a fixed order.
- **Nunito 600, 700 and 800, self-hosted** (`@fontsource/nunito`, Latin and Latin Extended), precached with the
  app, so it still makes no font requests and works offline.
- **All text tokens meet WCAG AA** (4.5:1) on every surface they are used on, in both themes; measured,
  not estimated.

## Tooling

- **Playwright drives an installed browser locally** (Edge by default, `PW_CHANNEL=chrome` to switch) and
  Playwright's own Chromium in CI. No browser download is needed on the development machine.
- **Unit tests run in `Asia/Tashkent` (UTC+5).** A regression to UTC-based day boundaries then fails
  loudly, because 00:30 local time is still "yesterday" in UTC.

## The curriculum (milestone 2)

- **Block A carries the study, block D carries the builds.** The brief lists the same engine work twice: in
  block A ("2D rigid-body engine", "OpenGL mini-engine") and again as numbered monthly projects, which belong
  to block D. Planning each build in both lanes would double its hours. So block A holds the courses, books
  and lecture series of graphics, simulation and gamedev, and block D holds the builds. A capstone is the one
  exception: it spans both lanes and states its share of the hours in each (`hours:` in `plan.yaml`), the only
  case where the validator allows an item to appear twice.
- **Five capstones, not two.** The brief names the Stage 1 capstone (35) and the final capstone (57-59) but
  also asks for "every year a capstone". Years 1, 2 and 4 got one each, written in the same spirit: an arcade
  game in C whose opponent you trained, a networked physics sandbox, and an instrumented control rig with a
  digital twin. They are the most editable thing in `/data`.
- **Two items moved a phase later than the brief's table.** K&R C moved from Y1-P1 to Y2 (block B there came
  to 142% of its hours) and MIT 8.01 from Y1-P1 to Y2 (block E came to 175%). Both still precede everything
  that needs them. Four lanes are still over budget: Y1-P0 block E at 120%, and block D in years 2 to 5 at
  110-122%, because that is what the brief's own project list comes to. The forecast surfaces it and the
  re-plan button is the remedy; nothing was quietly dropped to make the numbers look right.
- **Stage 2 projects that need no hardware say so** with `parts: []` rather than by leaving the field out, so
  "nothing to buy" is a statement in the data and a genuinely missing parts list still warns.
- **Weekly mini-builds are never on the plan.** They are a pool of 135 cards that "Pick my next project" draws
  from; `validate:data` rejects a weekly card that appears in `plan.yaml`.
- **Units are fetched, not typed.** `npm run fetch:units` reads each course's own syllabus page
  (`scripts/lib/unit-sources.ts` records which page and how to read it) and writes
  `data/units/<resource-id>.yaml`. Only titles and links are taken; no course content is copied. The files are
  committed and safe to edit by hand, as long as unit ids stay stable, because progress is stored against them.
- **The unit checklists load separately.** They are two fifths of the payload and are only needed once an item
  is opened, so the Vite plugin emits them as `virtual:atlas-units` and the app imports that dynamically.
  Every resource keeps a `unitCount`, which is all a progress bar needs.
- **Alternatives are kept, not chosen for you.** Where the brief says "X *or* Y" (MIT 6.041 or Stat 110, 3.091
  or 5.111, CCNA or Network+, SolidWorks or Autodesk, 2.004 or 6.302), both are in the library, one is on the
  roadmap, and the other says in its summary that it is the alternative.

## Backups, reviews and offline (milestone 8)

- **Backups are canonical JSON.** Keys are sorted and rows come in key order, so a given state always serialises
  to the same bytes. "Export, reset, import gives the same file" is then tested literally, and two backups
  can be compared with `diff`.
- **Import replaces; it does not merge.** Merging two histories has no right answer for a day logged in both.
  The file is checked against the schemas first and written in one transaction, so a bad file changes nothing.
- **The GitHub token never enters a backup**, and reset leaves it in place. Backup files get emailed and
  committed. The token lives in its own table, which export skips.
- **A new version waits for the user.** The service worker does not skip waiting on install. A banner offers
  Reload, so code never changes under an open form or a running timer.
- **The service worker ignores `Vary` when it reads its cache.** Every precached file has a content hash in its
  name, so the header adds nothing. Honouring it broke offline start on any host that sends `Vary: Origin`,
  because Vite loads every script and stylesheet with `crossorigin`.
- **The monthly review suggests a re-plan but never applies it.** Re-planning stays one deliberate action on
  the Roadmap, where its diff is shown first.
- **A completed review keeps a snapshot of its numbers.** Plan edits and re-plans change what "the target"
  means, and a review should read the same a year later.
- **The backup reminder counts from the first log, not from install.** With nothing logged there is nothing to
  lose, so it stays quiet.

## GitHub and the learning log (milestone 9)

- **The calendar is fetched as the token's owner (`viewer`), not by username.** There is no name to mistype.
  The login GitHub returns is stored in settings, so it travels with a backup, but the token does not.
- **A token is checked before it is stored.** A token GitHub refuses is never saved, so the app never holds a
  credential that does not work.
- **GitHub's levels, Atlas's layout.** The calendar uses GitHub's own quartile levels, because contributions
  are not minutes and mapping one onto the other would invent precision. It is drawn with the Atlas grid over
  the Atlas range and week start, in the same colour ramp, so a column means the same week in both.
- **At most one request per plan year per hour**, and none for days that have not happened. The cache is
  what makes the panel instant and keeps it readable offline. It lives with the token in the local-only
  table and goes with it.
- **The learning log is written from a backup.** The logs live in the browser, where a Node script cannot
  reach them. The export is already the one way out, and the script reads it with the app's own validation.
- **Public by default means reflections are opt-in.** The log is meant for a public repository.
  Reflections and energy ratings are personal, so they need `--reflections`.
- **Only days with time logged get a file.** A freeze day, or a note with no minutes, is not work, and the log
  exists to show honest work.
- **The script never deletes.** A file whose day lost its time is listed for the user to remove. Deleting
  from what may be someone's published history is their call.
- **Reset keeps the GitHub settings, as it keeps the token.** Otherwise Settings would show a token connected
  to no account name.

## Polish (milestone 10)

- **Shortcuts are single keys, and can be turned off.** The brief asks for `t`, `r`, `/` and `l`. Single-
  character shortcuts can be triggered by speech input, so WCAG 2.1.4 asks for a way to turn them off. The
  switch is a `meta` row, so it needs no schema change and travels with backups.
- **`/` finds the nearest search.** A page with a search box focuses it; any other page goes to the Library's,
  the app's general search.
- **`l` logs on the day in view.** From another page it opens Today; on Today it keeps the date you are
  looking at, so a missed day can be filled in with the keyboard too.
- **The accessibility audit is part of the tests.** axe runs on every page in both themes with WCAG 2.2 AA and
  best-practice rules, and a violation fails the suite. That keeps the pass from being a one-off.
- **The heatmap is one tab stop.** A year of days as separate tab stops is unusable from the keyboard.
  Movement follows the grid: up and down are days, left and right are weeks.
- **Screenshots come from a backup, not from code that reaches into the database.** The demo history goes in
  through the same Import as a user's file and is checked by the same validation. The demo invents no links,
  which is why its one finished-looking game is still in progress: done needs a repository.

## GitHub Sync (section 4.10)

- **One token for Sync and the calendar.** The brief has the calendar use "the same token", so the calendar
  switch moved into Settings > Sync. A calendar-only token saved before Sync keeps working, with a button to
  remove it.
- **The token's expiry is typed in.** The brief asks for a renewal reminder. GitHub sends the expiry in a
  `GitHub-Authentication-Token-Expiration` header, but its CORS `Access-Control-Expose-Headers` list leaves it
  out (checked against api.github.com on 2026-10-08, both on a plain response and on a preflight), so a page
  cannot read it. Settings has a "Token expires on" field instead. The reminder shows in Settings two weeks
  ahead and on Today's foot one week ahead. The code still reads the header, and fills the field by itself if
  GitHub ever exposes it.
- **The outbox holds file paths, not file contents.** The brief describes queue entries as `{path, content,
  baseSha}`. Here content is rendered when the flush runs, so twenty ticks of one checklist make one blob, and
  the base sha lives in one cache of the repository's tree. Each entry has a sequence number, so a flush clears
  only the entries that did not change again while it ran.
- **Stamps are added in one place.** A Dexie middleware stamps `updatedAt` and `deviceId` on every write to a
  synced table and queues the files it touches, so no screen has to remember to. Imports and pulls write
  "silently": no stamps, no queue.
- **The Git Data API for every commit but the first.** It cannot write to an empty repository, so an empty one
  first gets its README through the contents API, alone. After that, a flush is blobs, one tree, one commit and
  a fast-forward of the branch. If another device moved the branch in between, the sync starts again from the
  pull, up to three times.
- **A pull reads one tree and only the files whose sha changed.** The brief polls `state/` and the current
  month of `log/`. One recursive tree listing covers the whole repository at the same cost, so a day edited on
  another device last month arrives too. Requests skip the browser's HTTP cache, so a poll never sees a branch
  head older than the one just pushed.
- **Merging a day needs more than `updatedAt` per entry.** Entries get ids. A removed entry leaves a tombstone
  with a time, so "removed here, edited there" goes to whichever happened later; tombstones older than 60 days
  are dropped. Each day-level field (reflection, energy, freeze, done blocks) records when it changed, so a
  reflection written on the phone survives a timer stopped on the laptop. Ties go to the larger device id, so
  both devices pick the same winner. A skewed clock can make an older edit of one entry win; it never loses an
  entry only one side has.
- **On a device's first sync, the repository wins for settings and plan.** Otherwise a new phone would push
  its defaults over the settings it came to fetch. Item states still merge record by record.
- **Reset, with Sync on, resets this device only.** Nothing is deleted on GitHub: the next sync brings it all
  back. Deleting the data on GitHub is a job for GitHub. An import, with Sync on, replaces the repository's
  state with the file's on the next flush, which is what "Replace" says; the old state stays in Git history.
- **Commits are authored with the no-reply address** (`id+login@users.noreply.github.com`), which GitHub
  always counts on the contribution graph, unless the token may read the account's emails, in which case the
  primary verified one. Settings can change it.
- **Generated files are only written.** The Markdown twin of each day and the project pages are rebuilt from
  the JSON. Reviews are Markdown with the record as JSON front matter (valid YAML), so they read back exactly.
  The day twins include reflections and energy: the data repository is private, unlike the public learning log.
- **Project pages are named by project id** (`projects/w-c-ring-buffer.md`), not by title, so a curriculum edit
  that renames a project does not rename its file.
- **What stays on the device:** the timer, the keyboard-shortcut switch (the `meta` table), the token, the
  calendar cache and the sync state. None of it is in a backup or the repository.
- **A file Atlas cannot read is reported, not retried.** It is named in Settings and fetched again only when it
  changes on GitHub. A day file deleted on GitHub is deleted on the device too, unless the device has edits to
  it waiting.
- **Failures stay quiet.** A rate limit pauses sync until `X-RateLimit-Reset` or `Retry-After`. A refused token
  stops automatic syncs until "Sync now" or a reconnect. Offline, changes wait. Each is one phrase in Settings,
  and a quiet line on Today when changes cannot go out or have waited over two minutes.
- **`npm run test:sync` works on a throwaway branch** of a repository the user names, created from its default
  branch and deleted afterwards, pass or fail. It refuses an empty repository, because the first commit of a
  repository cannot be cleaned up. It refuses to run when `CI` is set, and reads the token from the
  environment only. `--fake` runs the same cycle against the in-memory GitHub of the tests.
- **The tests' GitHub is a small in-memory Git** (`tests/fake-github.ts`): real blob shas, made-up tree and
  commit shas, a branch that moves only by fast-forward, and api.github.com's real CORS headers in the browser
  tests, so the expiry header is hidden there as it is in production.

## The redesign (section 4.0) and the mockups

The redesign prompt and `Atlas Mockups.html` arrived together, and the user's instruction was that the UI must
be the same as the mockups. Where the two disagree, the mockups win, and each such case is listed here.

- **Square checkboxes.** Section 4.0 describes check circles; every mockup frame draws a box with softened
  corners. The box is what ships (24 px in clay).
- **The heatmap's day cells are smaller than 44 px.** Thirty weeks fill the card, as in the mockup, so a cell is
  about 9 px. The grid is one tab stop with arrow keys, and the last seven days open from 48 px rows under it.
  The tap-target test leaves the grid out for that reason.
- **The five-year map is its own page,** at `#/plan/map`, opened from "Five-year map" under the Timeline. The
  mockups draw it at desktop width; on a phone it scrolls sideways inside its frame, and the page itself never
  does.
- **Roadmaps sit at the top of Credentials,** not in a fifth segment. The brief asks for Plan > Roadmaps, and
  the mockups' segmented control has four segments, which is all that fits on a phone. A roadmap is mastered
  through its credential row, so Credentials is where it belongs.
- **The Library lists resources and papers, not projects.** The mockup's Library has no projects; they live in
  Plan > Projects and open from there.
- **Screenshot baselines are compared locally, not in CI.** `tests/e2e/screenshots.spec.ts` compares Today,
  Activity, Plan and Library in both themes, and the five-year map, with `screenshots/`. The pictures are drawn
  by Edge on Windows, and Linux renders the same text a pixel differently, so CI skips the comparison while
  every other test runs there. `npm run screenshots` redraws them.
- **Names in lists come from `short`.** The mockups say "ML Zoomcamp" and "18.06 Linear Algebra" where the
  catalogue's titles are "DataTalksClub Machine Learning Zoomcamp" and "MIT 18.06 Linear Algebra (Gilbert
  Strang, Spring 2010)". Resources may carry a `short` name, used on Today, Plan and the Library; sheets and
  search keep the full title.
- **The backup reminder is gone from Today.** The 2026-10-07 brief removed it. With Sync off, Settings says
  "Not backed up", once, as the brief asks, and still says when the last export was.

## Flexibility (the briefs of 2026-10-16)

The flexibility brief makes the day, the plan, the pace and the layout editable. The decisions below are the ones
it left open; `briefs/PLAN.md` records how the three briefs of that day fit together.

- **Work happens on the session branch.** This round was built in a cloud session that may push only to
  `claude/new-session-2cjn2k`. The stages (`flex`, `practice`, `catalogue`) are consecutive commits there, and
  local branches of those names mark where each ends. Merging into `main`, which triggers CI and the Pages deploy,
  waits for the owner's go-ahead.
- **Block ids are open-ended.** Any short capitalised id is a block (`/^[A-Z][A-Z0-9]{0,7}$/`): A to E stay the
  curriculum's lanes, P is Practice, and blocks the user adds take the next free letter. The authoritative data
  model in `src/seed/model.ts` had `'A' | … | 'E'`; it says `string` now, with a note. A to E cannot be deleted
  (the curriculum plans into them); taking one out of a template leaves it out of those days.
- **Tracks can be the user's own** (`my-…`), created as a `custom` plan patch. They take the colour of the first
  track in the colour slot chosen for them.
- **Settings version 2 is a superset of version 1, upgraded when read.** A version 1 file (from IndexedDB, a
  backup or another device's `state/settings.json`) still parses; `upgradeSettings` fills the new fields from
  `data/settings.yaml`. The Full day template lists its blocks without minutes, so it takes each block's own:
  someone who had set block D to 150 minutes keeps 150 minutes.
- **A schedule change never rewrites a day already past.** Editing templates, the weekly pattern or a block's
  minutes or weekdays first keeps a copy of the schedule as it was, "valid until yesterday"
  (`settings.schedule.history`), once per day. Pace and pauses carry the date they start. Changes to one date
  (Change today) name that date. So the streak, the heatmap and reviews of past days read the plan those days had.
- **Today's changes live in settings, by date** (`schedule.days`). They are small, they sync with the rest of the
  schedule, and keeping them is what keeps the past's plan. Only today and later days can be changed.
- **The streak rule** is "60 minutes or 50 % of the day's planned minutes, whichever is lower". A day that plans
  nothing (a rest day, a template with no blocks) counts if it reaches 60 minutes and otherwise holds the streak:
  it neither breaks it nor spends a freeze, and is never "at risk".
- **The heatmap is relative by default**, as the brief asks: 0, under 25 %, under 60 %, under 100 % and 100 % of
  the day's plan. A full 8-hour day is still the top step, so the redesign's acceptance test holds. A day that
  planned nothing falls back to the absolute thresholds. Settings switches back to minutes.
- **The forecast walks the future plan.** A lane's remaining hours are spent day by day at what the schedule

## Updating safely (pull request #1)

- **What existing users have** is the build of 2026-10-08: IndexedDB at Dexie version 2 (not 3), settings without a
  version field, plan.json version 1, backups of format 1. `tests/fixtures/live-2026-10-08/` was made by that build's
  own code, and `tests/unit/live-upgrade.test.ts` upgrades it: the database to version 5 with nothing lost (the old
  plan-item edits become patches and the plan reads the same), settings to version 2 with the user's values kept, the
  data repository into a new device and back out in the new formats.
- **Restoring an older backup kept its plan edits only by accident of the database upgrade.** Restore wrote the old
  plan-item overrides straight in, and the plan no longer reads those: they were silently lost. Restore now converts
  them to patches the same way the database upgrade does (`withLegacyPlanEdits`). Found by the fixture test.
- **A device still on the older build cannot read** state/plan.json version 2, state/settings.json version 2, a day
  with a Practice entry, or a review whose numbers include P (checked with that build's own parsers). When it changes
  one of those, it writes its own older copy over the newer one. It can still read everything else this build writes,
  and never touches practice.json or challenges.json. An old build cannot be changed after the fact, so the newer build
  defends itself (`src/sync/downgrade.ts`): a version 1 plan over local patches is merged record by record; settings
  without a version never replace version 2 settings; a day that lost entries in blocks outside A–E (with no tombstone
  for them) is merged, not taken; a review keeps the block numbers it would lose. Each time, the merged file goes back
  to GitHub and the device shows "A device with an older version of Atlas changed your data on GitHub … reload to update
  them". A device that is not upgraded takes nothing from settings it could not read: its own setting changes made
  in the meantime are dropped in favour of the newer settings, which is the one loss left, and it is visible in the
  notice. Old devices update on their next load (the service worker's update prompt).
- **A challenge's solution link is stored in `demoUrl`**, not in a new field, so items.json stays readable by older
  devices (their schema is strict and would reject the whole file).

## The timer and the layout fixed (pull request #10)

**The timer bug.** Two causes. The big counter showed the current run only (`now − startedAt`), so Stop then Start
began again at 0:00 while the time was in the log. And every stop rounded to whole minutes, so a session under 30 s
logged nothing ("0 h of 2 h", "0 h today") and each switch could lose up to half a minute.

- **Totals:** the counter, the Now card's "x of 2 h", its progress, the header and Blocks today all use one total
  per block for the date: the logged minutes, the carried seconds, and the running timer's part of that date.
- **The carry:** a log entry stays a whole number of minutes (an older device rejects anything else when it
  syncs), so a stop logs the whole minutes and keeps the leftover seconds on this device, keyed by date, block and
  item. The next stop on the same key adds them back. Carries older than two days are dropped. The carry does not
  sync: it is under a minute, like the timer itself.
- **One transaction:** a stop writes the log entries, the carry and the cleared timer together, so a reload or a
  closed tab never keeps half of it. Starts run one after another, so A → B → A within a second neither loses nor
  doubles a session.
- **Formats:** "12 m", "1 h 05 m", and "0 m" only when nothing is logged (it read "0 h"). The counter is m:ss under
  an hour and h:mm:ss from an hour.

**Today:** one header row (the mark, Atlas, "Sun 18 Oct", and the change, settings and theme knobs, 44 px each);
another day adds a Back to today chip under it, so the row still fits 360 px. The Now label never says "running"
(the header and Stop do). The two Now buttons share the row equally; below 380 px the second shows only its icon
and keeps its name for screen readers. On another day the primary is **Calendar**. The week strip is blue for any
past day with time logged (it showed only days that counted for the streak), pressed in for one without, ice for
today, with a ring on the day being viewed.

**Bottom padding** is the tab bar's 68 px, the 24 px it floats above the edge, 24 px of air and the safe area, in
the shared screen layout. It was already 120 px plus the safe area; the reported overlap on an Android phone did
not reproduce in Chromium at 360, 390 or 412 px, and a layout test now checks it on Today, Calendar and the Library.

**Calendar on phones:** under 600 px it opens on the day. The grid starts at the first block of the days shown (on
today, around the current time), at 56 px an hour, so a 15-minute step is 14 px. The week view's events are filled
in their tone with the icon and, from 48 px tall, a short label (ML, GD, HF, Code, Math, Proj, keyed by block id so
they match the daily schedule's names); pressed-in "still to come" stays in the day view, which has room to say so.
The date range sits on the title's row between "Calendar" and the arrows.

**Library:** a start month is short ("Feb"); Up next shows the month its phase starts and never "Now". Projects is a
full-width row under the shelf grid.

**Two races found on CI.** (1) A switch writes the other block's stop and then the new start; the new timer took its
start from the moment of that second write. A test that moved the clock between the two writes lost a block's
minutes (on a phone it would only shift the start by milliseconds). The tap's moment is now both the stop and the
start. (2) After a jump, the tech doc's bar ignores scroll events for 600 ms while the smooth scroll settles. A scroll
that ended inside that time left the bar on the part jumped to. The bar now reads the position once the time is up.
Both have tests that failed before the fix.

## The daily blog helper (pull request #12)

**Atlas does not hold the post.** The user writes it in their own blog through Pages CMS. Atlas keeps only whether
the day was posted, as `blog/<date>.json` (`posted`, `at`, and `editorLinkUsed` when Open my blog was used in that
tab first, `auto` when the detection found it). Undo writes `posted: false` instead of deleting the record, so the
undo reaches the other devices like any newer write. Devices on an older Atlas never read `blog/` (they pull only
the paths they know) and never delete it (a push sends only the device's own queued files).

**This device's blog settings.** The editor link and the detection switch live on the device (meta, like the token),
not in `state/settings.json`: a new key there would make an older device's strict parse of the file fail. Set once
per device.

**The editor link.** Prefilled with `https://app.pagescms.org`, the only address Atlas knows; the user pastes their
posts collection's own link. Atlas accepts https links only and never makes one up.

**What you did today.** Time comes from the day's log. What was ticked comes from a journal of ticks kept on this
device (meta `tickLog`, 60 days): item states record which units are done, not when. Items finished that day are
known on every device from their synced `doneAt`; units ticked on another device show there only. A tick is listed
under the block whose time was logged on its item, else the block that plans it that day; a unit with a known title
shows by its title, a milestone as "One finished small game · 2 ticked".

**The outline.** One `##` heading per block with time logged, then its ticks as bullets, then "What I learned:" and
"Stuck on:" to fill in, under each block (each course has something to say), and once on a day with nothing logged.

**The writing streak.** Days in a row with a post marked, ending today, or yesterday while today's is still to
write. A day with no study and no post neither counts nor breaks it, the way a rest day spares the study streak; any
other missed day ends it.

**The reminder.** From the moment the day's last block (by its start time) is marked done or its timer stopped with
time on it, or from the end of the last block (22:00), whichever is first; gone once posted. A row, never a pop-up.
The optional 22:00 notification is not built, with the block-start reminders (pull request #11): a web app cannot
notify reliably while closed.

**Detection.** The blog is `LinusIp/abdullohshavkatjonov`. Its Pages CMS config (`.pages.yml`, read on 2026-10-18)
has a `posts` collection at `_posts` with a `date` field; the files are Jekyll's `YYYY-MM-DD-title.md`. Atlas lists
`_posts` through the contents API and looks for a name starting with the date, once an evening (the first time Today
is open from 21:00) and whenever the blog screen opens on today. Only GETs. The repository is public, so any token
can read it: a fine-grained token always has read access to public repositories, and with no token at all the read
goes unauthenticated. If the read fails (a private blog the token was not given), the switch stays off and says how to

**Calendar.** A day view on today or before has a Blog button, "Blog ✓" once posted, which opens that day's screen.

## The ML Zoomcamp lesson checklist (pull request #13)

**Generic.** Any resource can have a lesson checklist: `data/units/<id>.json` in the shape of the Zoomcamp file
(`resource`, `playlist`, `modules[].items[]` with `kind`, `video`, `notes`, `optional`, `outdated`, `hours`,
`status`). The validator turns it into ordinary units (the module's title as the section, its id as the group, the
notes as the unit's link), so everything that already reads units (Plan, the blog, sync) keeps working. The old
`ml-zoomcamp.yaml` ("module N of 10") is gone; progress is stored against the new ids (`1.7`, `1.hw`, `12.p1`).

**What counts.** Videos, homework and projects that are neither optional nor outdated: 97 videos and 110 units for
ML Zoomcamp. "x of 97 videos done" counts videos. NOW is the first counted unit not ticked, in file order, so
ticking a module's last unit rolls over to the next module. Optional units can be ticked, never become NOW, and do
not count.

**The pace.** 15 minutes a video, a homework's or project's `hours` from the file or else 2 h and 20 h, 30 minutes
for anything else. The course's `estHours` is the sum over the counted units (rounded), and its progress is the share
of those hours ticked, exactly (no 5 % floor for "started"), capped below done until the course is marked done.

**The seed.** The file's `"status": "done"` items (1.1 to 1.6) are ticked once per device (meta `checklistSeeded`),
and only into an item state with none of the checklist's units ticked, so a tick is never overwritten. On a device
with no item state for the course the write is silent and dated 2000-01-01: older than any real edit, so a first
sync takes the repository's state over it. Where the course was already started (an item state from before the
checklist), the ticks are added as an ordinary edit, stamped now, so they reach the other devices. Item states merge
per record, newest wins, as before: a tick on one device and a note on another made between two syncs keep the later
record.

**The Now card.** Its window is the current module from two units before NOW to the module's end, homework
included, one line per row, no play knob on a lesson already done (as in the mockup). The full list (Show all
modules, and the Library sheet) keeps the knob on every video. The second button is "Play 1.7", named by its own
text; the row's knob is "Play 1.7 Introduction to NumPy". The card is taller than before: like the mockup, its
buttons sit under the list.

**Links.** The links come from the user's file, not invented. `check:links` now also requests the video links, and
CI runs it on the checklist as a report (never failing the build), as the last step of `verify`, even after a
failure, with only the links that did not answer printed, so its summary ends the job's log. Its run here found one broken notes link, 11.7's
`07-kserve-eks.dm`, fixed to `.md` after the checker fetched it (200, the right page). YouTube cannot be reached
from the build container; the runner's report covers the videos.

**Live check.** The deploy's live check opens the course's Library sheet and requires 1.1 to 1.6 ticked, 1.7 not,
and 1.7 marked Now.

## The sleep log (pull request #14)

**The night's date.** A night belongs to the date it ended on, so last night shows on today. The screen enters two
times of day: the bedtime is on the evening before when it is later in the day than the wake time (23:00 → 05:40), and
on the wake-up date when it is earlier (00:30 → 06:00). Times are stored as local wall-clock times without an offset
("2026-10-17T23:00"), and the minutes are the difference of the two wall clocks: exact in Asia/Tashkent, which has no
daylight saving.

**Validation.** Wake after bed is required (the two-times entry always gives it; a synced or imported record is
checked by the schema: positive minutes). Under 1 hour or over 16 hours, Save turns into "Save anyway" with "Are you
sure? That is 16 h 40 m." above it: asked, never refused. One record per wake-up date: saving replaces it, with Undo.

**Sync.** Table `sleep` (Dexie version 7), one file per night, `sleep/<date>.json`, merged by the newer `updatedAt`
like the blog marks. Backups carry it.

**Defaults.** The usual bedtime is the median of the last 7 nights logged (times before noon count as after midnight,
so 23:30 and 00:30 give 00:00), 23:00 before the first. The wake time is now rounded to 5 minutes before noon on that
morning, else the median wake time, 06:00 before the first.

**The time input.** A native `<input type="time">` lies unseen over the sunken time: a tap opens the phone's own
picker, a keyboard types into it, and the well shows the time in 24 hours whatever the browser's locale. Calling
`showPicker()` from a separate button was tried and dropped: the picker it opens keeps the typing from reaching the
field. The sleep log writes times in 24 hours, as the mockup does, whatever Settings › Layout's clock says.

**The bedtime tile.** "For 8 h by 06:00": the goal before tomorrow's first block (its start in the day's layout, so it
follows a changed schedule); today's first block when tomorrow has none, 06:00 when neither has.

**Settings.** The goal (6 to 10 h in half hours) and the morning question are this device's (meta `sleepSettings`),
like the blog settings: they shape how this phone asks, not the data.

**The morning question.** On today, while no night is logged for it, before 12:00 and while it is on: a row under the
week strip ("How did you sleep?", Log sleep), not a pop-up.

**Today still fits, once answered.** The morning question costs a row (114 px with its tile), and Today had about 15
px to spare above the tab bar at 390 x 844. The brief asks for the row at the top, so it stays there for the few
minutes it is up: it goes once the night is logged or at 12:00, and "Today reaches Later today at 390 x 844" is
checked with last night logged. Putting the question inside Later today (like the blog reminder) would keep the fit
but hide it below the Now card exactly when it is asked.

**Today.** The Slept tile takes the place of "Foundations I · days left"; the days left moved to Plan's header ("Year
1 · Foundations I · ends 31 January · 105 days left").

**Activity.** A Sleep tile under the two numbers, linking to the screen: the average of the last 7 nights and the last
30 as small bars (the same scale and colours as the week chart). Its accessible name says the average, how many of
the 30 nights are logged and their range. Neither the tile nor the screen gives advice or scores.

**The chart's text.** The week chart is one image whose name lists each night ("Monday 7.0 hours, Tuesday not
logged, ...") and the goal; the bars and numbers are hidden from screen readers.
